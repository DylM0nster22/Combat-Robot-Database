Designing a 1 lb plastic-antweight vertical spinner: a numeric walkthrough

Design Walkthroughs

Plastic-antweight rulesets generally restrict most structural/weapon material to non-metal (motors, fasteners, bearings, and small hardened inserts are usually still allowed in metal, but frame, armor and the bulk of the weapon typically must be plastic). This changes the weight-math story significantly compared to the metal-weapon walkthrough above.

1. Weapon in plastic. A nylon disc (density ~1.15 g/cm^3) at the same 30 mm radius / 6 mm thick geometry as a metal example has volume = pir^2t = pi0.030^20.006 = 1.70e-5 m^3 = 16.96 cm^3, giving mass = 16.961.15 = 19.5 g -- versus 133 g if the identical shape were cut from steel (16.967.85). Using formula-moi-solid-disc: I = 0.50.01950.030^2 = **8.775e-6 kg*m^2**, roughly a quarter of the metal build's compound-weapon MOI.

2. Pick a realistic plastic-weapon tip speed. Plastic teeth/edges are far more failure-prone at high tip speed than hardened steel, so plastic-class spinners are usually run slower: target ~80 ft/s (24.4 m/s) rather than 150+ mph. omega = v/r = 24.4/0.030 = 813 rad/s = 7,767 rpm.

3. Stored energy. KE = 0.58.775e-6813.3^2 = 0.58.775e-6661,457 = 2.9 J -- an order of magnitude below the metal build's 71 J. This is normal and expected: plastic-antweight spinners are built for board-clearing and harassment rather than devastating single hits; wedges and control tactics carry more relative weight in this class.

4. Drivetrain is unaffected by the material rule (motors, wheels, gearboxes are usually still metal/off-the-shelf), so the same drivetrain numbers as the metal build apply: 11,000 rpm base motor, 20:1 gearbox, 32 mm wheel -> 0.92 m/s top speed (formula-top-speed-drivetrain).

5. Weight budget shifts toward armor/frame. Because plastic structure needs much greater thickness for the same stiffness (see formula-sheet-weight-per-area: a 6mm HDPE panel weighs 57 g vs 81 g for a thinner 3mm aluminum panel of the same area, but the HDPE panel needed 2x the thickness just to compete structurally), a realistic plastic-ant budget is: weapon 15% = 68 g; drive 20% = 91 g; armor/frame 40% = 182 g; electronics 15% = 68 g; fasteners 10% = 45 g -- notice armor/frame roughly doubles its percentage share versus the metal build's 30%.

Final spec: ~20 g nylon disc weapon, ~7,800 rpm / 24.4 m/s tip speed, ~2.9 J stored, same drivetrain as a metal build, but with 40% of weight (182 g) committed to armor/frame instead of 30%.
