Charging Practice: Balance Charging, Rates, Storage Voltage, and Chargers

Charging

antweightplastic-antweightchargingbalance-chargingstorage-voltagechargers

Balance charging is mandatory, not optional, for any multi-cell LiPo/LiHV pack. A balance charger monitors each cell individually through the balance lead and tops each one off in step, preventing any single cell from being overcharged while others lag behind — the single biggest safeguard against the cell-imbalance failures that lead to puffing, capacity loss, or fire. Never charge a multi-cell pack through the main leads only.

Charge rate: the safe default is 1C (a 450mAh pack charges at 0.45A) for best pack longevity, though most modern packs and chargers support faster charging (2-5C on some packs) when time is tight, at some cost to long-term cycle life. Always charge to the chemistry-correct cutoff: 4.20V/cell for standard LiPo, 4.35V/cell for LiHV — charging an LiHV pack on a standard LiPo setting leaves capacity unused, while charging a standard LiPo pack to LiHV voltage is a real overcharge/fire risk.

Storage voltage: for any pack that won't be used within the next few days, discharge or charge it to roughly 3.8V/cell (an acceptable range is about 3.7-3.85V/cell). This keeps the cell chemistry in its most stable state for long-term storage, unlike leaving a pack fully charged (accelerates degradation) or fully depleted (risk of over-discharge damage) between events.

Chargers combat builders commonly use: ISDT's compact chargers (e.g. the ISDT 608AC, an 8A/200W AC/DC unit supporting LiPo/LiHV/Li-ion/LiFe/NiMH from 1-6S with an XT60 port) are popular for their small size and multi-chemistry support — useful given how many different chemistries show up across an antweight builder's battery drawer. HOTA's D6 Pro is well regarded for balance accuracy and a high 1.6A/cell balance current, which matters on the very small capacities antweight packs use (a weak balance current struggles to actually balance a 300mAh pack in reasonable time). SkyRC's T1000 and similar units combine charge and discharge functions in one box, letting a builder both charge match packs and discharge them to storage voltage after an event without a separate tool.

Parallel charging safety: charging multiple packs simultaneously through a parallel charging board is common for efficiency, but every pack in the parallel group must be the same cell count, a very similar state of charge, and in good health — a damaged or significantly more depleted pack in the group can pull a dangerous surge of current from its healthier neighbors the moment the group is connected, before the charger even starts. Never parallel-charge a pack you have not individually voltage-checked, and never mix cell counts or chemistries in one parallel group.
