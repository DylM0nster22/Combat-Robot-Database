TPU Armor and Flexible Structures — Popular, But Check the Rules

Meta

plastic-antweightmetatpuarmororg-variance

A frequently repeated piece of combat-robotics wisdom is that flexible TPU armor 'beats' rigid armor in small weight classes: a rigid PLA/ABS panel absorbs an impact by cracking or shattering, transmitting shock into whatever it's bolted to, while a TPU panel deflects under the same impact and springs back to shape, spreading the load over time instead of failing catastrophically. This is genuinely true as material science, and it's why TPU shows up widely in the general (metal-legal) antweight and beetleweight scene as flexible skirts, shock-mounted panels, and wheel tires.

But plastic antweight is where this advice needs a hard caveat. TPU is not on the SPARC 4.4.1 baseline approved-materials list (PET, PETG, ABS, PLA, PLA+) at all, and SCAR's Plastic Ant FAQ goes further and explicitly bans TPU by name, including for custom wheel hubs — one of the few uses that might otherwise seem like an obvious carve-out under the wheels/tires exemption. This makes TPU a genuine org-to-org trap: a builder who read general combat-robotics forum advice praising TPU armor, without separately checking their specific plastic-ant event's material list, can show up with a rules-illegal robot.

What plastic ant builders do instead, within the approved list:

  • PETG over PLA/PLA+ for anything expected to take a direct hit, since PETG's more ductile failure mode (bending/cracking slowly) approximates some of TPU's shock-spreading benefit without leaving the approved material family.
  • Print-in-place flexures — thin, deliberately flexible sections of an otherwise rigid PETG/ABS part, designed as living hinges or shock-absorbing tabs — get some of TPU's benefit from a rules-legal material by using geometry (thin walls) rather than a different filament chemistry to create flex.
  • Where TPU IS explicitly permitted (some events leave it un-addressed and treat organizer-approved 'additional plastics meeting the spirit of the rules' as covering it, or grant a specific written exception), builders do use it for wheel tires and armor skirts, getting the full flex benefit — but this should never be assumed without confirmation.

Bottom line: TPU's flex-beats-rigid advantage is real physics, but its legality is the single most variable rule across plastic-ant events reviewed for this file. Always check the specific event's ruleset and FAQ, not general combat-robotics community wisdom, before designing TPU into a competition build.
