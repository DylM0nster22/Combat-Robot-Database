Getting metal parts made

Fabrication

antweightplastic-antweightfabrication

For a 1 lb robot you need very little metal, which changes what is economic.

Waterjet. Cuts anything, with no heat-affected zone. This matters for hardened weapon steel — a laser's heat can alter the temper at the edge — and for titanium. Slight edge taper on thick material, irrelevant at antweight thicknesses. The usual choice for titanium armour and hardened weapon parts.

Fibre laser. Excellent on steel and thin stainless, good on titanium with the right setup, fast and cheap. The heat-affected zone is small but real; for a hardened weapon, prefer waterjet.

CO2 laser. Cuts acrylic and wood. Does not usefully cut metal at hobby power levels. Note that it does not cut polycarbonate or UHMW acceptably — PC chars and yellows at the edge, UHMW melts and re-welds behind the beam. If your design needs those materials, plan on sawing, routing or milling.

CNC milling. Necessary for anything with 3D form — a hub, a pocketed chassis, a contoured wedge. More expensive per part than 2D cutting, so design in flat plates where you can.

Send-out services. The standard workflow without a shop: draw it, export DXF or STEP, upload, wait one to two weeks. Two practical cautions at 1 lb scale: check the service's minimum hole diameter and minimum web width, because antweight parts routinely fall below what larger-format services will quote; and check which metals they actually stock — titanium in particular is not offered everywhere.

Practical advice. Order spares in the same job. The setup cost dominates and the marginal cost of a second or third copy of a small part is small. Order a spare weapon and a spare wedge plate with every order; you will use them.
