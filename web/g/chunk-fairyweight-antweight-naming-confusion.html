Why 'Antweight' Means Two Different Weights (US vs UK)

Weight Classes

fairyweightantweightantweightuk-vs-usnamingunits

One of the most common points of confusion for a new builder researching antweight combat robots online is that 'Antweight' does not mean the same weight everywhere. In the United States, and under the SPARC standard used by most North American events (RoboGames, Motorama, Robot Ruckus, Bot Blast), Antweight is a 1 lb (454 g) class. In the United Kingdom, under the Antweight World Series (AWS) rules run through Bristol Bot Builders, 'Antweight' is only 150 g for a roller-locomotion robot (225 g if it uses non-rolling locomotion) — roughly a third of the US weight.

This 150 g UK number is exactly what US/SPARC terminology calls 'Fairyweight.' So a build video, forum post, or parts list that says 'antweight' could be describing a robot three times heavier than another build also labeled 'antweight,' purely because of which country's rules the author is using. The practical effects run deep: a US 1 lb antweight can carry a meaningfully bigger battery, a heavier drive motor pair, and thicker armor than a UK 150 g antweight, so motor/ESC/battery recommendations, weapon tip speeds, and even typical arena sizes differ between the two 'antweight' scenes even though the robots look similar in silhouette.

When reading any antweight rule, spec sheet, or parts recommendation, the single most important thing to check first is whether the source is using US/SPARC units (1 lb / 454 g class) or UK/AWS units (150 g class, with a separate lighter Fleaweight at 75 g and Nanoweight at 25 g below it). Below UK antweight, AWS runs Fleaweight (75 g roller / 113 g non-roller) and Nanoweight (25 g roller / 38 g non-roller) — classes that have no direct US equivalent at all, since the US ladder jumps straight from a much heavier 1 lb antweight up to 3 lb beetleweight with nothing smaller. A builder targeting an international audience, or ordering parts from a UK antweight supplier for a US 1 lb build, should always convert and double-check the actual gram/pound figure rather than relying on the class name alone.
