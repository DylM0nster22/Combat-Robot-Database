The weapon-motor size ladder: 11xx to 28xx for antweights

Weapon Motor Fundamentals

antweightplastic-antweightbeetleweightsize-laddermotor-selection

The FPV drone industry has produced an enormous range of brushless outrunner motors at low cost, and combat builders have adopted a de-facto size ladder from that catalog rather than buying purpose-made weapon motors for every build:

11xx-14xx (1104, 1106, 1204, 1306, 1404, 1408) -- plastic antweight and fairyweight territory. These are whoop and toothpick drone motors, 9-30g, with 1.5mm shafts and M2 mounting screws. They suit small plastic-ant vertical spinners and paddle/beater weapons under roughly 50-80g total weapon assembly weight. 1306 and 1408 in particular are extremely common because they are cheap, widely stocked in many KV options (2200-6000kv depending on size), and light enough to leave most of a plastic ant's tiny weight budget for drive and armor.

18xx-22xx (1806, 2004, 2205, 2206, 2207, 2306) -- the antweight bread and butter. This is where the overwhelming majority of competitive 1 lb antweight spinners live. These motors have 3-5mm shafts, M3 mounting (mostly 16x16mm), weigh 28-36g bare, and are sold in KV ranges from roughly 1300 to 2700 across 4S-rated windings. A 2205 or 2207 direct-driving a 3-4 inch disc or short bar at 4S is probably the single most common antweight weapon configuration in the hobby.

25xx-28xx (2504, 2508, 2806, 2812, 2814) -- heavy antweight / light beetleweight crossover. These are long-range and heavy-lift drone motors, 50-115g, with 5-6mm shafts, run at lower KV (800-1900), and are usually belt-driven rather than direct-drive because their bulk and shaft size don't always suit small antweight hub geometry. They show up on the heaviest 1 lb builds that sacrifice armor/drive weight for weapon torque, and are more at home on light beetleweights (2 lb / 3 lb classes) than on a weight-conscious antweight.

As a rough allocation, a competitive 1 lb antweight vertical spinner typically spends 60-120g of its 454g total weight on the weapon motor plus hub/disc, which naturally steers most builds toward the 18xx-22xx bracket: heavy enough for real torque and thermal margin, light enough to leave a meaningful armor and drive budget.
