Building an antweight with no machine shop

Fabrication

antweightplastic-antweightfabricationgetting-started

You do not need a mill or a lathe to build a competitive antweight. Here is the realistic path.

What you actually need to own:

  • A cordless drill or a small drill press
  • A set of drill bits in metric sizes up to about 5 mm
  • A hacksaw or a small bandsaw
  • Files — a flat and a round, at minimum
  • Metric hex drivers (1.5, 2, 2.5 mm covers M2 through M3)
  • A soldering iron, solder and heat-shrink
  • Digital calipers
  • A small scale accurate to 0.1 g

That is roughly a couple of hundred dollars and it is enough.

What you send out. Anything that needs to be cut accurately from metal: wedge plates, weapon discs, armour panels. Draw it, export a DXF, and upload to an online laser or waterjet service. For 1 lb parts the cost is usually modest because you are buying almost no material — the setup dominates. Send several spares in the same order; the marginal cost is small.

What you print. Chassis, mounts, spacers, wheels, non-structural covers, and in plastic ant, everything. A printer changes the economics of the hobby more than any other tool.

What you buy finished. Motors, gearboxes, wheels, ESCs, receivers, batteries, bearings, fasteners. There is no reason to make any of these.

The practical assembly approach. Design so the robot bolts together from flat plates and printed parts. Avoid anything needing a tapped hole in metal if you can — use through-bolts with nylocs. Where you must tap, an M3 tap and a tapping drill are cheap and hand-tapping aluminium is easy.

The order things usually go wrong. Holes drilled slightly off position, which a round file fixes. Parts that do not fit because you forgot fastener head clearance. And a robot that comes in 20 g overweight because printed parts weigh more than the CAD says. Weigh as you go.
