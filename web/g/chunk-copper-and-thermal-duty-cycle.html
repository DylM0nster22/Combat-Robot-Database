Duty cycle, stator saturation and continuous-vs-burst rating for weapon motors

Failure Modes & Durability

antweightplastic-antweightduty-cyclethermalstator-saturation

FPV drone motors are rated by their manufacturers around a duty cycle that looks nothing like combat use: a drone motor spends the overwhelming majority of its flight time at a fairly steady, moderate current near hover/cruise, with occasional short bursts to full throttle for maneuvers. A combat weapon motor instead spends most of a match either coasting at low current (weapon at speed, only overcoming bearing/aero drag) or pinned at very high current for a few hundred milliseconds to a few seconds during spin-up and post-stall recovery -- a duty cycle the manufacturer's continuous-current rating was never validated against.

This matters because a motor's practical current limit is really a thermal limit: current produces I^2R copper loss as heat in the windings, and the motor's continuous-current rating is roughly the current at which heat generation matches the motor's ability to shed heat to the air around it in steady state. Combat weapon use routinely exceeds the continuous* rating for the duration of a spin-up or stall-recovery event, relying on the stator's thermal mass to absorb that heat without reaching damaging temperatures before it can dissipate during the following lower-current period. This works fine for an occasional stall, but a match with many rapid stall/recovery cycles (grappling, being pinned, repeated wall hits) can accumulate heat faster than it dissipates, eventually cooking the winding insulation or demagnetizing the rotor even though no single instant exceeded the motor's absolute peak rating.

Stator saturation is a related but distinct limit: iron cores can only support so much magnetic flux before they saturate, beyond which additional current stops producing a proportional increase in torque and instead mostly generates extra heat with little mechanical benefit. Bigger, higher-quality stators (thicker laminations, better core steel) saturate at higher currents, which is part of why purpose-built or premium weapon motors can safely run at currents that would saturate (and overheat) a cheap generic drone motor of the same nominal size.

Practical implication for motor choice: for weapon use, favor motors with generous stator volume relative to your target torque/current (i.e., don't run a motor at the very top of its rated current as your normal operating point), since the thermal and saturation margin left over is what actually determines how many hard stalls a motor survives across a full tournament rather than just a single match.
