Fasteners that actually hold

Assembly

antweightplastic-antweightfabricationreliabilitymaintenance

Losing a fastener is the single most common way an antweight stops working, and it is almost entirely preventable.

Use alloy steel, not stainless. Grade 12.9 alloy steel is roughly twice the tensile strength of common A2 stainless. In a 1 lb robot the fasteners are small enough that this difference decides whether a bolt shears. Stainless is for things that need to not rust; nothing on a combat robot needs that more than it needs strength.

Threadlocker on everything. Blue, medium strength, applied to the male thread. Red is effectively permanent without heat you should not apply near printed parts. This is the cheapest reliability improvement available and it takes minutes.

Nylocs where you have through-holes. A nylon-insert nut genuinely resists vibration. It is not a substitute for threadlocker in a tapped hole — different situation, different solution.

Never thread into plastic. Heat-set inserts where you need a reusable thread in a printed part, captive nuts where you have room, through-bolts where you can. A thread cut into plastic survives a handful of assemblies and no real impacts.

Match the driver properly. Most stripped fasteners are stripped by a slightly worn or slightly wrong-size hex key. Buy a decent set, replace them when they round off, and carry spares of the sizes your robot uses.

Size sensibly. M3 for anything carrying load — weapon hubs, armour mounts, motor mounts. M2 for electronics trays and light covers. Below M2 the head strips before the thread yields, so there is no point.

The maintenance habit. Between every match, go around every accessible fastener with the driver and check it is tight. It takes two minutes and it prevents the failure that most often ends an event. Vibration from any spinning weapon works on every joint continuously; assume things are loosening unless you have checked.
