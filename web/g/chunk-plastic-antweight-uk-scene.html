The UK Plastic Ant Scene and the 150g Naming Trap

Community

The UK combat robotics scene uses different weight-class names than the US, and this creates a real point of confusion for anyone researching "plastic antweight" online. In the UK, the term "antweight" traditionally refers to the 150g class, not the 1 lb / 454 g class that US builders mean when they say "antweight" or "plastic ant." The US 1lb bracket has a rough UK-terminology equivalent, but the plastic movement is active at both scales in the UK: there are 150g plastic/printable antweights as well as heavier printable classes, and community-shared designs freely cross weight brackets in spirit even though they aren't the same competition category.

A good concrete example is Legally Distinct, a free, open-source 150g UK antweight drum-spinner design shared on Thingiverse by community builder Blessy, explicitly described as "heavily inspired by" an established UK antweight called Siren. This shows the same open, iterative design culture found in the US 1lb plastic ant scene — successful designs get openly documented, and other builders print, remix, and re-share variants — just operating at the UK's 150g weight bracket rather than the US 1lb bracket most SPARC-derived rulesets target.

Practical implication for researchers and builders: when reading a UK build log, forum post, or Thingiverse description, always check the stated gram weight rather than assuming "antweight" means 454g. A UK 150g printable drum spinner and a US 1lb / 454g SPARC-legal plastic ant are built to different weight budgets, different arena sizes, and sometimes different club-specific material rules, even though both fall under the broad 'plastic ant' / '3D-printed insect-class robot' umbrella this file otherwise treats as one class.

That said, the underlying design lessons transfer well across the naming gap: both scenes favor thick printed drum/disc weapons over thin cutting bars, both rely on PETG/PLA+/ABS as the workhorse filaments, and both treat a cracked printed part as a five-minute reprint rather than a season-ending failure.
