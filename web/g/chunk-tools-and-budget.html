Tools and budget: what to buy first

Getting started

antweightplastic-antweightgetting-startedfabrication

An honest ordering for someone starting from nothing. Prices are indicative and vary by region.

Tier 1 — you cannot build without these:

  • Digital calipers
  • A scale accurate to 0.1 g (you will be fighting for single grams)
  • Metric hex drivers, 1.5 / 2 / 2.5 mm
  • Soldering iron, solder, heat-shrink
  • Side cutters and small pliers
  • A cordless drill and metric bits

Tier 2 — the first real capability upgrade:

  • A 3D printer. This changes the hobby more than anything else on the list. It makes plastic antweight possible outright and makes metal-ant prototyping nearly free.
  • A LiPo charger with balance charging, and a LiPo-safe bag
  • A multimeter

Tier 3 — quality of life:

  • A small drill press (accuracy of hole position improves enormously)
  • A bench vice
  • A Dremel or similar rotary tool
  • A heat-set insert tip for your soldering iron

Tier 4 — genuinely optional:

  • A bandsaw, a small mill, a lathe. These are lovely and none of them are necessary. Send-out cutting services cover what you need for 1 lb parts.

Where the money actually goes. For a first antweight, the robot itself is often cheaper than the tools: motors, an integrated ESC/receiver board, a battery and a transmitter come to a moderate sum, and the transmitter is reusable across every robot you ever build. Buy a decent transmitter once rather than a cheap one twice.

The one thing people under-buy. Spare parts. A robot with no spares is a robot that fights until the first failure. Budget for multiples of the parts that touch the opponent from the start.
