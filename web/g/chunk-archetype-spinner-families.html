The three spinner families and what separates them

Spinners

antweightplastic-antweightspinnermetaphysics

Every spinning weapon falls into one of three families defined by the axis it turns about, and the axis decides nearly everything else.

Vertical spinners (discs, bars, drums, beater bars, eggbeaters) turn about a horizontal axis across the robot. Their damage vector is upward. This is the strongest vector at antweight because a 454 g robot has so little mass that a modest hit lifts it clear of the floor, and a robot in the air is not driving, not defending and not scoring. Their weakness is engagement: the weapon has to reach the opponent, and a good wedge prevents that.

Horizontal spinners (bars, discs, shells) turn about a vertical axis. Their damage vector is sideways, and their reach is enormous — the weapon sweeps a circle far larger than the robot. They are devastating against wheels and against other spinners' weapons. Their weakness is that a wedge simply passes underneath the sweep, and every hit throws them backwards as hard as it throws the opponent.

Undercutters are horizontal spinners mounted low enough to strike below an opponent's armour line. They exist specifically to beat the wedges that beat ordinary horizontals. The cost is precision: the weapon runs millimetres off the floor and must stay there after being hit.

The practical consequence is a rock-paper-scissors that is not quite balanced. Vertical spinners beat wedges and control bots. Wedges beat horizontal spinners. Horizontal spinners and undercutters beat vertical spinners by attacking the weapon and the wheels. Because the wedge is cheap and reliable, the meta settles on vertical spinners that carry wedges — which is why undercutters are the interesting counter-play.
