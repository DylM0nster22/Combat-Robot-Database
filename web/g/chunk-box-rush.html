The box rush: executing it and defending it

Driving

antweightplastic-antweightdrivingstrategy

The box rush is charging the opponent the instant the match starts, before their weapon reaches speed. It is the highest-value opening in antweight because spinners are helpless during spin-up and spin-up takes seconds.

Executing one. You need a robot that is dangerous immediately — a wedge, a control bot, or a spinner with a very short spin-up. Line up square to the opponent, commit fully on the count, and aim to arrive under them before their weapon is useful. Against a spinner, getting under it during spin-up often wins the match outright: you pin it, it never spins up, and you bank three minutes of control.

Defending one. Several layers, use as many as apply:

  • Spin up before the count if your event's rules allow it. Many do. This removes the entire vulnerability.
  • Start at an angle, not square on. A rushing opponent has to change direction to reach you, and changing direction costs them speed.
  • Reverse off the line. Backing away buys a second of spin-up and forces the rusher to cross the arena again.
  • Build a safe front. If your robot's front face is safe to be hit at any weapon speed — thick armour, wedgelets, nothing fragile exposed — a box rush costs you position rather than the match.
  • Shorten your spin-up. This is a design fix: less weapon inertia, more motor torque, higher cell count. A weapon that is up in one second cannot be box-rushed meaningfully.

The judgement call. If you are a spinner facing a known rusher, the question is whether to meet them or run. Generally: run. Reversing and circling while your weapon comes up costs you a few control points and preserves your entire game plan. Meeting a rush at half weapon speed loses the weapon.
