Antweight World Series (AWS): The UK's Antweight-Scale Standard

Major Rulesets

fairyweightantweightawsukrulesetantweight

The Antweight World Series (AWS), administered in association with Bristol Bot Builders, is the primary UK/European ruleset for antweight-scale combat robotics, and it is built around a materially different, much lighter class ladder than the US SPARC standard: Antweight at 150 g (roller) / 225 g (non-roller), Fleaweight at 75 g / 113 g, and Nanoweight at 25 g / 38 g. There is no US equivalent to Fleaweight or Nanoweight at all — the US ladder's lightest class (1 lb antweight, or SPARC's own separately-named Fairyweight at 150 g) doesn't go nearly as small.

AWS's radio rules reflect the UK scene's longer history with analog and early digital RC systems: it explicitly permits 27 MHz, 40 MHz, 418 MHz, 433-434 MHz, 868 MHz, and 2.45 GHz, a wider allowed-frequency list than most modern 2.4GHz-only US rulesets, likely preserving compatibility with older UK-legal surface radio-control gear still in circulation at hobbyist level. Battery rules cap voltage at 24V and explicitly prohibit any battery containing liquid acid or free electrolyte (ruling out traditional wet-cell chemistries entirely, though this is largely moot in an era of LiPo/LiFe dominance).

Weapon safety rules under AWS are detailed: weapons must be remotely deactivable, any pressurized system (CO2, pneumatics) is capped at 100 psi (7 bar), and any detachable weapon component must remain tethered to the robot with a maximum tether length of 3 feet (914 mm) to prevent it becoming an uncontrolled projectile. The banned-weapons list closely tracks what other rulesets prohibit but is phrased slightly differently: glue or sticky-pad weapons, fluid-based weapons, string/entanglement weapons, flame-based weapons, using electricity itself as a weapon (e.g. deliberately shocking an opponent), explosive weapons, rotating weapons judged prone to shattering (a specific concern given how small and fast antweight-scale spinners can spin), and magnetic/inductive systems intended to interfere with an opponent.

Match structure: 3-minute maximum matches, a 10-second immobility countdown that ends a fight if one robot shows no controlled movement, a 20-second cap on pinning before release is required, and an automatic loss for any robot that touches the bottom of an arena pit. Clusterbots that mix roller and non-roller sub-units use an explicit formula — Roller weight allowance = Roller class limit − (2/3 × total non-roller weight) — to fairly balance the roller/non-roller weight bonus across a multi-unit entry rather than letting teams stack cheap non-roller mass to inflate their effective roller allowance.
