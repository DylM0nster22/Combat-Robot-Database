The case for building a control bot

Control

antweightplastic-antweightcontrolgetting-startedstrategy

New builders almost always want a spinner, and almost always would learn more from a control bot. The argument:

Most antweight matches are lost, not won. At 1 lb the commonest outcomes are a loose bolt, a dead battery, a thrown wheel, a weapon that would not spin up, and a robot that failed inspection. A control bot has fewer things that can do any of that.

Driving is the dominant skill at this level and you cannot practise it with a broken robot. A control bot finishes every match, which means every match is practice. A fragile spinner gets ninety seconds of experience and then goes back in the pit.

The weight budget is generous. With no weapon, no weapon ESC and no weapon battery load, you have something like 75% of your mass for drive and armour. That buys a drivetrain that out-pushes everything in its bracket and armour that survives what does hit you.

Spinners beat themselves against good armour. A significant fraction of a spinner's energy goes back into the spinner. A hard, compliant, well-braced target is a genuine counter to a fragile high-energy opponent.

The honest counter-argument: control bots must win on judges' decisions, which are not guaranteed, and a control bot that cannot generate damage will lose to a competent vertical spinner that also carries a wedge. That is real. But as a first or second robot, the learning rate is much higher, and the drivetrain and armour knowledge carries directly into whatever you build next.
