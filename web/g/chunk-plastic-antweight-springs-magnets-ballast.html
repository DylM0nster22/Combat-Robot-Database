Springs, Rubber Bands, Magnets and Ballast Legality

Rules

plastic-antweightrulesspringsmagnetsballast

Magnets: Universally banned when used 'to enhance traction or downforce' (SPARC 4.4.2, SCAR ruleset). This is a general antweight-class rule that also applies to plastic ants — the concern is that magnetic downforce would let a light robot behave like a much heavier one against a steel arena floor, which most 1lb-class arenas do use. Small magnets used for non-traction purposes (e.g., holding an access panel shut) are generally not the target of this rule, but builders should confirm with the organizer if in doubt.

Springs: SPARC's general springs-and-flywheels section (13.1) explicitly exempts robots in the 12lb class or smaller — which includes every plastic ant — from its stricter large-spring loading/remote-actuation requirements, as long as 'safe operation and good engineering' are followed. This means a plastic ant can use a small pre-loaded spring (e.g., in a snap-action flipper or lifter) without needing the remote-arming mechanism required of larger robots' big weapon springs. The spring itself, if it's a coil or leaf spring, is metal and falls under the same 'non-plastic mechanical part' logic as a bearing or shaft — it's not armor or the primary weapon surface, so it's allowed.

Rubber bands: Not specifically addressed in any plastic-ant ruleset reviewed, but by the same logic as springs, a rubber band used as a small mechanical actuator (e.g., snapping a lifter arm closed) is a functional non-plastic part, not armor or a weapon surface, and would be treated as legal under the standard exemption language. Builders relying on this for a weapon mechanism should still get organizer sign-off given the general 'contact us if borderline' guidance every ruleset repeats.

Ballast: Not explicitly named as banned or allowed in the plastic-only material lists, but ballast is understood by the community to fall under the same test as everything else — if it's simply dense material added to bring the robot up to the full 454g weight limit and it isn't functioning as armor, it's legal regardless of material (a small chunk of steel ballast, screwed into an internal cavity and not exposed as a surface, does not 'enhance structural integrity, armor the robot, or enhance any weapon'). Many plastic ant builders instead use printed ballast (a solid, high-infill printed plug) simply because it's zero-cost and trivially plastic-legal, avoiding the question entirely.
