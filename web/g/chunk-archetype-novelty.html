The novelty archetypes and whether they are worth it

Other

antweightplastic-antweightnoveltystrategy

Some archetypes exist more for the challenge than for the win rate. An honest assessment of each at 1 lb:

Melty brain — genuinely clever and genuinely hard. You need an accelerometer, custom firmware, and a tolerance for a robot that translates slowly and loses all its energy whenever it manoeuvres. Worth building if the software is the point. Not worth building to win.

Walkers — the weight bonus is real and large, commonly up to double the class limit for a true walker. The catch is that qualifying is strictly judged and the mechanism consumes much of the bonus it earns. Confirm your event's exact bonus and qualification criteria before designing around it.

Shufflers — a partial bonus for a simpler mechanism, but some rulesets classify shufflers as wheeled and award nothing. Check first; the entire value proposition depends on it.

Multibots — splitting 454 g gives you numbers and positioning at the cost of each robot being individually fragile. Rules differ sharply on whether both robots must be immobilised to lose. Read the specific ruleset.

Crushers — scale badly downward. The force needed to pierce armour demands gearing that makes the mechanism slow and heavy, and a 1 lb robot lacks the traction to hold an opponent still while it works.

Saws — spectacular and ineffective. Combat armour resists cutting, and the weight spent on an arm plus a blade motor buys less damage than the same grams in a disc.

Tracked robots — look ideal for pushing, disappoint in practice. A thrown track is an instant loss and antweight-scale tensioners are hard to make robust.

None of these are bad projects. They are bad first projects, and several are bad competitive choices. Build them because you want to solve their specific problem, not because you expect them to win a bracket.
