Repeat Robotics: Brushed and Brushless Planetary Drive for Antweights

Drive Motors

antweightplastic-antweightdrive-motorrepeat-roboticsbrushlessplanetarygearbox

Repeat Robotics built its antweight product line around one core idea: replace the simple spur gearbox found in motors like FingerTech's Spark line with a multi-stage, all-metal planetary gearbox, which generally handles shock loading and delivers torque more efficiently than a spur train of the same size. Their catalog spans both brushed and brushless motor options in the same 16mm-class form factor antweight builders are used to.

On the brushed side, the Mini Brushed Mk2 (sold as the "Mini Pro Brushed" for around $25) offers a straightforward drop-in alternative to a Silver Spark, with the planetary gearbox as its main differentiator, at a modest price premium over FingerTech's spur-geared option.

On the brushless side, Repeat pioneered selling off-the-shelf 16mm brushless gearmotors specifically for combat robots — before this, brushless drive meant a builder sourcing a bare FPV-style outrunner motor and building or 3D-printing a custom gearbox around it. The Mini Brushless line went through several revisions: early Mk3/Mk4 versions used a custom-wound ~3500kv 1104-size motor; the current Mk4.1 upgrades to a larger-stator 1106-size motor for more torque and power capability in the same envelope, paired with a 28.5:1 two-stage all-metal planetary gearbox and a hardened 4mm output shaft, weighing about 24g complete. Because it's brushless, it needs a reversing brushless ESC rather than a simple brushed H-bridge — Repeat sells a matching ESC designed specifically for their motors — but in exchange gets higher efficiency, no brush wear, and a rated 2S-4S voltage range that lets a builder retune top speed just by changing battery cell count rather than swapping hardware. A higher-spec "Mini Pro 1106" variant is also sold at roughly double the price for builders wanting the premium tier.

Repeat's catalog also includes larger motors like the Compact Brushless 1806 (marketed as the lightest brushless beetleweight drive motor on the market), useful context for how this same drive-pod architecture scales up a weight class, though it's oversized for a standard 1 lb budget. The general trade-off across Repeat's line versus FingerTech's Spark motors is: more durability and (for brushless) more speed/efficiency, at a real cost premium and, for brushless specifically, added ESC complexity and the sensorless-cogging caveats that come with any FPV-derived brushless motor run at very low speed.
