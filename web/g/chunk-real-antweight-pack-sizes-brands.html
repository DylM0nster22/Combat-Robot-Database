Real Antweight Pack Sizes and Brands: What Builders Actually Buy

Product Landscape

antweightplastic-antweightproduct-roundupbrandspack-sizing

Antweight LiPo/LiHV packs cluster tightly around a handful of capacity/cell-count combinations because they have to fit inside a 1 lb chassis alongside a receiver, ESCs, and a weapon system, while still delivering multi-minute runtime.

180-300mAh 2S is the smallest practical antweight size, common on the lightest control bots and early builds. Example: the Tattu 300mAh 2S 45C (7.4V nominal, ~23g, 48.6x18.8x13.9mm, JST-SYP) — a widely stocked pack that crosses over directly from micro FPV. The Palm Beach Bots Palm Power 2S 300mAh 65C is a combat-market-specific equivalent with a JST connector and separate balance lead.

300-450mAh 3S is the most common antweight spinner size. The CNHL MiniStar 3S 450mAh 70C (11.1V, XT30) and its LiHV sibling, the CNHL LiHV MiniStar 3S 450mAh 70C (11.4V nominal, same 70C/XT30), are directly comparable real products that illustrate the LiHV capacity gain in the same physical pack. Palm Beach Bots' Palm Power 3S 450mAh 45C (XT30) sells for $14.99 as of recent listings — a useful real price anchor for this size class.

350-550mAh 4S is used for higher-voltage plastic antweight and metal antweight builds; the GNB LiHV 4S 350mAh 70C (15.2V nominal, XT30) is a real example sized for this niche.

Brand landscape: Tattu and Gens Ace are sister brands from the same manufacturer (Grepow group) and their packs of matching spec are usually near-identical. CNHL (China Hobby Line) is a major supplier of small-format packs, including combat-labeled MiniStar and LiHV MiniStar lines. GNB (Gaoneng) is notable for actively marketing LiHV packs directly to the antweight community, not just FPV. Lumenier and RDQ Series (RaceDayQuads' house brand) are FPV-first lines that get cross-shopped into antweight builds because their micro-pack sizes overlap almost exactly with antweight needs, though specific SKU weights/prices for combat use are less consistently documented than the combat-market-specific brands. FingerTech Robotics' Galaxy line and Palm Beach Bots' Palm Power line are combat-market-specific options sold directly by long-standing antweight vendors, bundled with kits like the Viper V3.

Across all of these, weight for a given capacity/cell-count/C-rating combination is fairly consistent regardless of brand — a 3S 450mAh pack lands in the 30-40g range whichever manufacturer makes it — so the deciding factors in practice are connector type (JST for the smallest/oldest builds, XT30 once current climbs), C-rating credibility, and price/availability rather than dramatic weight differences between brands.
