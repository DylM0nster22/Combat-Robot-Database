Gyroscopic effects on a spinner-weapon chassis, and predicting 'gyro dance'

Gyroscopic Fundamentals

antweightplastic-antweightgyroscopicprecessionvertical-spinnerchassis-stabilityfundamentals

A spinning weapon carries angular momentum along its own spin axis. When the chassis carrying that weapon is itself rotated (yawed) by the drivetrain -- turning in place, or even just cornering hard -- that angular momentum reacts via gyroscopic precession, producing a torque about a THIRD axis, perpendicular to both the weapon spin axis and the turn axis. In a vertical spinner (weapon axis roughly horizontal, side to side), turning the chassis produces a precession torque that tries to pitch the front or back of the robot up -- the visible "gyro dance" or "gyro wobble" many builders describe.

The core relation is formula-gyroscopic-precession-torque: tau_precession = I omega_weapon omega_turn. Precession torque scales directly (linearly) with all three of weapon inertia, weapon speed, AND how fast the driver yaws the chassis -- meaning a robot that feels perfectly planted driving straight, or even turning gently, can suddenly start rocking the instant the driver spins hard in place at full weapon RPM.

Comparing against the chassis's own resistance to tipping (formula-tipping-moment-threshold, M_tip = W track_width/2) tells you whether a given turn rate is actually going to destabilize the robot. In the worked example (I = 3.25e-5 kgm^2, weapon at 20,000 rpm, 454 g chassis with a 90 mm track), a fairly modest 3 rad/s (~0.5 rev/s) in-place turn rate produces 0.204 Nm of precession torque against a 0.200 Nm tipping threshold -- meaning this specific robot is right at its tipping edge at that turn rate and weapon speed.

Predicting the safe limit directly (formula-max-safe-turn-rate-gyro): rearranging for the maximum safe turn rate gives omega_turn_max = M_tip/(I*omega_weapon) = 2.94 rad/s (~169 deg/s) for this example -- a concrete number a driver or an autonomous turn-rate limiter can be tuned against.

Practical mitigations informed by these formulas: widen the track/wheelbase (raises M_tip), lower the CG (raises the tipping angle per formula-cg-tipping-angle, giving more margin at the same M_tip), reduce weapon MOI or top speed if turning agility matters more than raw hitting power, or simply train the driver to turn in wide arcs rather than spinning in place whenever the weapon is at speed.
