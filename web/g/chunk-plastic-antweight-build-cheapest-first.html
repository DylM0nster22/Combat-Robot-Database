For a builder trying to spend as little as possible on a first legal, functional plastic ant (excluding a transmitter/charger they may already own, or plan to reuse across future robots), a minimal control-style build looks roughly like this. One-time reusable costs not counted above (a builder typically already owns these or buys them once for many future robots): a basic FlySky-compatible or similar hobby transmitter (~$35-45) and a LiPo-safe charger (~$20-30).

Note the large weight headroom: this minimal build uses only about 215g of the 454g budget. Because filament is so cheap relative to the total build cost, the single best 'free' upgrade for a first-time builder is to simply print the chassis with more walls and higher infill than the bare minimum — using the full weight budget costs perhaps an extra $1-2 of filament but meaningfully increases durability, since the marginal cost of plastic mass is far lower than the marginal cost of any other component in this class. A cheapest-build budget should therefore prioritize spending the saved money on better electronics reliability (a name-brand combo board over the cheapest option) rather than on unnecessary weapon parts, and spend the 'free' leftover weight budget on structural thickness rather than leaving it unused.

No weapon in this build — it is deliberately a bare-minimum control/wedge robot, both because it's the cheapest path to a functional, rules-legal, competition-ready first robot and because it avoids the weapon motor/ESC/shaft/bearing costs (which together typically add $35-50+ to a first build) entirely.
