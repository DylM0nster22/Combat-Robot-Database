Bite: definition, tooth-count tradeoffs, and interaction with opponent geometry

Bite

antweightplastic-antweightbitetooth-countwedgeground-clearance

'Bite' is how far a spinning weapon's tooth digs into an opponent's material during a single hit before the next tooth strike arrives -- it is a per-hit penetration depth, not a measure of total damage or total energy delivered over a fight.

The core relationship. As established in the design-math topic's bite-depth formulas (formula-bite-depth, formula-hit-frequency), bite depth per hit is set by closing speed divided by hit frequency, and hit frequency is weapon RPM times tooth count. At 20,000 rpm with a single tooth and 1.5 m/s of closing speed, hit frequency is 333.3 hits/second and bite depth works out to 1.5/333.3 = 4.5 mm per hit; adding a second tooth at the same rpm doubles hit frequency to 666.7 hits/second and halves bite depth to 2.25 mm.

Why fewer teeth means deeper bite -- and the real tradeoff. Fewer teeth means a longer time gap between successive strikes at a given rpm, so each individual strike has more time (and therefore more relative travel/closing distance) to dig in before the next tooth arrives. formula-wg-tooth-count-moi-equivalence makes the important complementary point: splitting the same total tooth mass at the same radius across more teeth does not reduce the weapon's total stored energy or tip speed -- it only redistributes how that fixed energy budget is delivered, as fewer/deeper hits or more/shallower ones. This is why single-tooth asymmetric discs remain the go-to choice for builders chasing single devastating hits (able to punch through armor a shallower multi-tooth hit would only dent), while multi-tooth discs suit builds prioritizing a steady, harder-to-avoid stream of contact and, generally, an easier balancing job since mass is spread more evenly around the rim.

Bite vs opponent wedge angle and ground clearance. Bite depth computed against a flat vertical face is an upper bound -- real opponents rarely present a flat vertical surface. formula-wg-bite-vs-wedge-angle models how a sloped wedge face reduces the normal (penetrating) component of a hit: a shallow 10-degree wedge preserves roughly 83% of the flat-face bite depth, while a steep 40-degree wedge lip cuts effective bite to roughly a third, and in practice the tooth is also likely to deflect and ride up a steep face rather than dig in cleanly. Ground clearance compounds this: an opponent's wedge that sits lower than the spinner's own ground clearance may never be reached by the weapon at all regardless of computed bite depth, which is exactly the gap undercutters are built to close (see term-ground-clearance-weapon and term-floor-interaction-undercutter).

Tooth profile's effect on bite. Bite depth from the RPM/tooth-count formula assumes a straightforward radial impact; formula-wg-bite-depth-rake-angle adjusts this for swept-back (raked) teeth, which engage a somewhat longer material path for the same computed bite depth but at lower peak force -- useful context when comparing why two discs with identical rpm, tooth count, and closing speed can still feel and perform differently depending on tooth profile (term-tooth-profile, term-rake-angle).
