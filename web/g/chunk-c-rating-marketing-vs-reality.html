C Rating: What the Number Means and Why It Lies

C Rating

antweightplastic-antweightc-ratingcurrentmarketing

A pack's C rating is a multiplier on its capacity: Max current (A) = Capacity (Ah) x C rating. A 450mAh (0.45Ah) pack rated 70C is claiming 0.45 x 70 = 31.5A continuous, and many packs also print a separate, higher "burst" C rating (often 1.5-2x the continuous number) for short spikes.

The uncomfortable truth is that printed C ratings on budget and mid-tier packs are marketing numbers, not lab-tested guarantees, and they have been for as long as small LiPo packs have existed. Manufacturers competing on a spec sheet have every incentive to print an impressive number, and there is no independent, universally enforced test standard behind most of them. Reputable brands (name-brand Tattu, CNHL's better lines, GNB) are generally closer to honest than unbranded eBay packs, but even they should be treated with a healthy discount, especially for continuous (not burst) draw.

A realistic antweight rule of thumb: for planning purposes, trust roughly 40-60% of a pack's printed continuous C rating from a mainstream combat/FPV brand, and treat the printed burst rating as, at best, a one-to-two-second spike capability rather than something the pack can repeat every few seconds for a full match. So a "70C continuous" 450mAh pack should be planned around as if it were really good for something like 13-19A of sustained current, not 31.5A.

How to compute what you actually need: estimate combined continuous drive+weapon current (see formula-power-budget-drive-weapon) and multiply by a comfortable safety margin (1.5-2x) to get your target continuous current requirement; separately estimate your worst-case simultaneous spike (weapon spin-up plus a hard driving input) for your target burst requirement. Pick a pack whose discounted continuous rating clears the continuous target and whose burst rating clears the spike target — not a pack whose printed continuous number merely looks bigger than your estimated draw.

In practice, for a typical antweight combined draw in the 10-20A continuous / 25-35A brief-spike range, almost any name-brand 3S pack rated 45C or higher in the 300-550mAh range clears this bar even after the realistic discount — which is why C-rating shopping matters less in antweight than pack capacity, weight, and voltage sag behavior (internal resistance) do. Chasing an ever-higher printed C rating on an already-adequate pack mostly buys marketing, not match performance.
