Exactly What Metal Is Allowed — Wording Across Organizations

Rules

Every plastic-ant ruleset carves out a near-identical exemption list, but it's worth reading the exact wording since enforcement details differ.

SPARC 4.4.2 (the most widely copied phrasing): "Non-plastic parts such as motors, electronics, axles, fasteners and adhesives can be any material, but cannot be used in such a way to enhance the structural integrity, armor the robot, or enhance any weapon." This is a functional test, not a parts-list test — the question inspectors ask is whether a given metal part is doing double duty as armor or a weapon surface, not simply whether it's metal.

SCAR's Plastic Ant FAQ gets more concrete, explicitly naming: metal bearings, axles, shafts and fasteners; COTS motor-mount adapters; and even metal servo horns/gears, provided the weapon is non-functional without its plastic parts (i.e., the metal gear alone couldn't act as the striking surface). SCAR also explicitly allows COTS wheel/tire components from vendors like FingerTech, Repeat Robotics, or Pololu — including their rubber, cast urethane, or foam tire materials — since wheels/tires are treated as outside the structural/armor/weapon restriction entirely.

Robot Ruckus adds a specific enforcement detail not spelled out elsewhere: where metal fasteners appear on the outer surface of the robot, 'they should be countersunk or shrouded' — i.e., a proud screw head sticking up from the shell could be argued to function as tiny armor or a weapon-like protrusion, so it must be recessed or hidden under a plastic cap.

Practical exemption checklist most events agree on:

  • Drive motors, weapon motors — any material (always metal in practice)
  • ESCs, receivers, wiring, connectors, switches — any material
  • Weapon shaft/axle — metal is normal and expected
  • Bearings — metal is normal and expected
  • Screws/fasteners/threaded inserts — metal allowed, but countersink/shroud outer-surface heads
  • Adhesives (super glue, epoxy used as glue, not as an armor coating) — any material
  • Battery — any material (never printed anyway)

What's not on this list — and therefore must be plastic — is anything that is itself a structural panel, an armor plate, or the actual striking face/body of a weapon.
