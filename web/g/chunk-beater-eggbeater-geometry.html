Beater bars and eggbeaters: geometry and practical differences

Weapon Forms

antweightplastic-antweightbeater-bareggbeatergeometry

Beater bars and eggbeaters are close cousins of the bar spinner, distinguished mainly by axis orientation and the number/arrangement of striking arms rather than by fundamentally different geometry principles.

Beater bar. Geometrically similar to a horizontal bar spinner but usually shorter, lower-powered, and mounted so it beats downward/forward into an opponent rather than delivering a single dominant cutting hit -- closer in spirit to a flail than a precision cutter. Beater bars on antweights are often a simple flat or slightly curved bar of modest length (well under a full-width bar spinner), prioritizing a fast, forgiving spin-up and reasonable durability over maximum single-hit energy, and are comparatively tolerant of imperfect balance because their tip speeds and stored energy are usually lower than a dedicated hard-hitting bar or disc.

Eggbeater. An eggbeater weapon uses two (or more) separate arms mounted on a shared vertical-ish or angled axis, positioned and phased so their swept paths overlap or interleave -- visually and mechanically similar to a kitchen eggbeater/whisk, hence the name. Each arm behaves like its own small bar spinner for MOI and tip-speed purposes (formula-moi-slender-rod-center and formula-tip-speed in the design-math topic apply to each arm individually, then combine), but the multi-arm layout increases hit frequency similarly to adding teeth to a disc, and the geometry must ensure the arms' swept circles don't collide with the robot's own frame or with each other through the full rotation. Balancing an eggbeater is more involved than a simple two-armed bar because the arms are often not a single rigid symmetric part -- each arm's mass and mounting must be checked individually as well as the assembly as a whole (see term-dynamic-balance).

Shared practical notes. Both forms tend to run at more modest tip speeds than a dedicated single-plane disc or bar, trading peak per-hit energy for a more continuous, harder-to-avoid threat and (for the eggbeater specifically) some self-righting utility from the weapon's shape and low ground clearance. Both are comparatively forgiving weapon choices for a first hard-weapon build, since their lower typical tip speeds put less extreme demand on balance tolerance and bearing selection than a full-speed disc or shell spinner.
