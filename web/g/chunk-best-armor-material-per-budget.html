Best Armor Material Per Budget

Comparative Selection

antweightplastic-antweightbudgetarmor-selectioncomparison

Armor material choice in 1 lb combat robots is almost always a three-way negotiation between weight budget, dollar budget, and the opponent field you expect to face. There is no single best answer, but the tiers below reflect how the community generally allocates spend.

Unlimited-ish budget (metal antweight, ~$50-150 spent on armor alone): Titanium Grade 5, 0.8-1.5mm. Best strength-to-weight available; the limiting factor becomes your wallet and your ability to cut/drill it cleanly, not the material's performance. A well-designed titanium-shelled antweight is close to the practical ceiling for that weight class.

Mid budget (~$20-50): AR400/AR500 steel wear plates on high-risk edges only (not a full shell — too heavy), or 1095 spring steel flexible skirts, combined with an aluminum (6061/7075) or G10 frame. This gets you real armor performance at a fraction of titanium's cost, accepting a weight penalty that has to come out of drive/weapon budget elsewhere.

Budget/plastic-antweight (~$0-20, often just filament already owned): thick-walled PLA/PLA+ shell (2-4mm walls, 40-60% infill) is the default and, per the community consensus, performs surprisingly well for its cost because it can be printed so much thicker than a metal budget allows. Layering a TPU 95A skin over a PLA or PETG structural shell is widely considered the current best plastic-antweight armor system — it costs barely more than PLA alone (roughly $25-30/kg for either) but adds genuine impact compliance that a rigid-only plastic shell cannot match.

The universal budget upgrade: regardless of tier, spending a few dollars on TPU 95A as an outer skin or edge-wrap over whatever rigid material you're using (titanium, aluminum, PLA) is one of the highest value-per-dollar armor upgrades in the class, because it adds impact compliance without meaningfully changing the rigid structure underneath.

What to avoid at any budget: acrylic and stock ABS sheet as a primary armor face — both are cheap but a false economy, since they crack/shatter rather than absorb impact, meaning a "budget" acrylic shell often performs worse than free scrap cardboard would in terms of actually protecting the internals (though obviously nobody should use cardboard either). Spend the same few dollars on PETG or PLA instead and get a materially tougher result for the same cost.
