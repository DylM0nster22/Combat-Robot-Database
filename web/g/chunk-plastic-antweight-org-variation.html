Org-to-Org Variation in the Plastic Ant Rules

Although nearly every plastic-ant ruleset in North America descends from the same source (Western Allied Robotics' 2015 original, absorbed into SPARC's template), real differences exist once you compare specific events:

The single most important variance to know: whether TPU and nylon count as legal 'plastic.' SCAR bans both by name. Other events that only quote the bare SPARC 4.4.1 list (which doesn't mention TPU or nylon at all) leave it ambiguous — technically neither is on the approved list either, so a strict reading bans them everywhere unless an organizer explicitly grants an exception. Builders should never assume a design that used TPU tires or a nylon gear at one event will pass technical inspection at another; always check the specific event's FAQ or ask the organizer directly, exactly as SPARC 4.4.3 itself recommends: 'Contact the event organizer ahead of time if you are not sure your robot meets the above definition.'

Online/remote plastic-ant competitions (where robots are shipped to a central venue and matches are run and streamed by event staff, a format popularized by NHRL for larger classes) generally adopt whichever local ruleset the hosting club already runs rather than inventing new material rules, so the same org-to-org variation applies to them too.
