Plastic Antweight: Rules, Materials, and Why It's the Best First Build

Weight Classes

Plastic antweight is a 1 lb (454 g) class variant that restricts the robot's structure to a defined list of 3D-printable/thermoplastic materials, with electronics, motors, fasteners, and bearings exempted from the material restriction. It was started by Western Allied Robotics specifically to lower the cost and skill barrier of entering antweight combat, and the rule has since been folded into the broader SPARC standard so it is recognized at essentially any SPARC-compliant US event.

Under SPARC's plastic-class rules, the approved structural materials are PLA, PLA+, ABS, PETG, ASA, and PET — all common, cheap, easy-to-print filaments available from any hobby 3D printer supplier. Explicitly banned are TPU (too soft/flexible to give a fair strength-per-weight comparison and complicates armor rules), nylon (much tougher and more expensive to print reliably), and any fiber-reinforced or composite filament such as carbon-fiber-loaded or fiberglass-loaded nylon/PETG, which would defeat the purpose of a low-cost, low-performance-ceiling class by letting well-funded builders buy their way to metal-like strength.

The practical effect of this restriction is a class where damage is dramatically lower than a comparable metal antweight: plastic armor cracks, chips, and deforms rather than punching through, plastic weapons tend to shatter rather than embed, and a whole robot can typically be printed and assembled for well under $50 in material cost, with the most expensive parts being the motor, battery, receiver, and ESC — all of which are reusable across many builds even after the printed chassis is destroyed. This is exactly why plastic antweight, alongside 150 g fairyweight, is the class most consistently recommended to first-time combat robot builders: mistakes are cheap, a printer replaces broken parts overnight, and the skills learned (weight budgeting, wiring, driving, basic weapon design) transfer directly to metal antweight and beetleweight later. Because the class shares the same 1 lb weight limit as regular antweight, plastic antweight robots are frequently run in the same event bracket as metal antweights, though many clubs and regional events (Robot Ruckus, Bot Blast, and others) also run a dedicated plastic-only bracket to keep matches fairer between plastic and metal builds.
