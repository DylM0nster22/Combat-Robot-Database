Motorama, Robot Ruckus, and Bot Blast: The US Regional Event Circuit

Beyond the handful of large, well-documented US organizations (NHRL, RoboGames, BattleBots), a wider circuit of regional, independently-run, SPARC-compliant events forms the backbone of where most antweight and plastic-antweight builders actually get their fight experience. Three names that come up repeatedly in the US hobby scene are Motorama, Robot Ruckus, and Bot Blast.

Motorama is one of the longest-running events on the US calendar, historically associated with Pennsylvania and more recently held in the Doswell, Virginia area, and is often treated as an early-season fixture that many builders use to debut a new antweight or plastic-antweight design before the rest of the year's events. Like most regional US events, it runs SPARC-compliant weight classes rather than maintaining its own separate rulebook, which is precisely the point of SPARC's existence: a robot built to spec for Motorama should need no changes to also compete at Robot Ruckus, Bot Blast, or a RoboGames combat bracket.

Robot Ruckus is a confirmed annual SPARC-compliant event co-located with Maker Faire Orlando at the Central Florida Fairgrounds (4603 W Colonial Dr, Orlando, FL). It splits its tournament format by class size: the insect classes (fairyweight, antweight, plastic-antweight, beetleweight) run double-elimination brackets, while the larger 12lb, 30lb, and 30lb-super-class divisions use a pools/Swiss-style fight format instead, reflecting how many more matches a small, cheap-to-repair antweight can realistically fight in a single day compared to a larger, slower-to-repair robot.

Bot Blast is a confirmed annual insectweight-focused event running a modified version of SPARC rather than stock SPARC. Its documented deviations are: a modified double-elimination format, a ban on autonomous robots (unlike RoboGames, which runs a dedicated autonomous division), an immediate knockout — at the presiding official's discretion — for any robot found with an exposed or damaged LiPo battery during a match (a notable safety escalation beyond baseline SPARC, reflecting how seriously the hobby treats LiPo fire risk), and simplified judging criteria relative to full SPARC judging guidelines. Bot Blast explicitly discourages 'rules-lawyering' loopholes that give an unsportsmanlike advantage, a philosophy stated directly in its published rules.

Both events, along with Motorama, illustrate why SPARC's existence matters in practice: a plastic antweight or antweight built once to SPARC spec can travel between Motorama, Robot Ruckus, and Bot Blast (and on to RoboGames or a regional NHRL-adjacent event) with at most minor, clearly-documented local adjustments (like Bot Blast's exposed-LiPo rule) rather than a full rebuild for each event's own from-scratch rulebook.
