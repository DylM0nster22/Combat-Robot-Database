Material Weight-Per-Area Table for 1 lb Budgeting

Weight Budgeting

antweightplastic-antweightweight-budgetingtablearmor-thicknessreference

One of the most useful numbers when designing a 1 lb (453.6g) antweight is grams-per-square-inch at a given thickness, since armor and chassis panels are usually sized by area, not just volume. Weight per square inch scales linearly with thickness and density: weight_g = density_g_cm3 x area_in2 x 6.4516 x thickness_mm / 10.

How to use this table: a 1 lb bot has roughly 400-420g left after drive/weapon/electronics for structure and armor (often much less — many antweights spend 150-250g on armor+frame combined). A titanium shell at 0.8mm covering ~40 in² of surface area (a small boxy antweight) costs roughly 40 x 2.29 = ~92g — a serious chunk of the budget, which is exactly why most titanium antweights use titanium only on the highest-risk faces (front/top) and cheaper/lighter materials (printed plastic, thin aluminum) elsewhere. The same 40 in² in 1mm PLA costs only ~32g, which is why plastic-antweight builders can afford much thicker walls (2-4mm) for the same weight a metal antweight spends on a thin titanium skin — printed plastic buys wall thickness (and therefore some stiffness and impact resistance) that a metal build cannot afford at the same weight.

Note that "100% infill PLA" is a simplification — most printed antweight parts use lower infill (20-60%) with multiple perimeter walls for a good strength-to-weight compromise, so actual printed-part weight per square inch is often 40-70% of the solid-material number above depending on infill/wall settings.
