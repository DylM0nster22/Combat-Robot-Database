Parallel Charging: Why It's Convenient and How It Goes Wrong

Charging

antweightplastic-antweightparallel-chargingsafetycharging

Antweight builders frequently own many small packs — several 300-550mAh cells for the main bot plus spares — and charging them one at a time on a single-channel charger is slow, so parallel charging boards (a small PCB with multiple balance/main connectors wired in parallel, feeding one charger channel) are a common convenience. Used correctly, parallel charging is safe and is exactly how many combat pit areas charge banks of small packs quickly between rounds.

The failure mode is specific and avoidable: when packs at meaningfully different voltages are connected together in parallel, current flows between the packs themselves the instant they're connected — before the charger has done anything — as the higher-voltage pack tries to equalize with the lower-voltage one. If one pack in the group is significantly more depleted, damaged, or has higher internal resistance than the others, this equalization current can be large and, in a worst case, generate enough localized heat to damage or ignite the weaker pack. This is why every credible parallel-charging guide insists on three checks before connecting packs together: (1) all packs must be the same cell count and chemistry, (2) all packs should be within about 0.1-0.2V of each other per cell before connecting, and (3) every pack must be visually and electrically healthy (no puffing, no damaged leads, no cells reading suspiciously low).

For antweight builders specifically, this matters because match-day packs often come back from a bout at very different states of charge — one pack might have fought a short match and returned at 70% capacity, another might have gone the full 3 minutes and returned near-empty. Tossing both into the same parallel board without checking voltage first is a common and preventable mistake. The safe habit is to check each pack's resting voltage individually (a simple LiPo checker or the balance port on a multimeter) before adding it to a parallel group, and to keep visibly damaged or hard-hit packs (see the mounting/impact-damage chunk) out of any parallel group entirely, charging them alone and watching them the whole time, or retiring them outright.
