The 1 lb Antweight Drive Motor Landscape

Drive Motors

antweightplastic-antweightdrive-motoroverviewfingertechrepeat-roboticsn-seriespololu

Antweight combat robots (1 lb / 454 g total weight) have a narrow but well-developed set of drive motor choices, almost all built around small brushed or brushless motors in the 12-16mm size range. The dominant, most trusted choice remains FingerTech's Silver Spark: a Mabuchi FK-050 brushed motor behind an all-metal 16mm spur gearbox, sold in nine stock ratios from a fast 11.1:1 to a crawling 600:1, all at the same ~29g weight and ~$19 price. Its budget sibling, the Gold Spark, uses the same base motor but a plastic final gear stage, trading some durability for a lower price (~$14.79) — fine for lighter-duty plastic antweights, riskier as the sole drive on a bot expected to take direct hits.

A newer and increasingly popular tier is Repeat Robotics' lineup: brushed and brushless planetary gearmotors that swap the Spark line's simple spur gearbox for a multi-stage all-metal planetary gearbox, which is generally more shock-tolerant and torque-dense for its size. Repeat's brushless "Mini" 1106 gearmotor (evolved through Mk3, Mk4, and Mk4.1 revisions) packages a custom-wound outrunner brushless motor directly into a 28.5:1 planetary gearbox with a hardened 4mm shaft, letting builders trade a simple brushed H-bridge for a reversing brushless ESC in exchange for higher efficiency and higher top speed at the same weight.

At the budget end of the market sit the generic N-series gearmotors (N10/N20/N30, 10-30mm motor cans) sold by dozens of overlapping AliExpress/Amazon/Banggood listings, often for a few dollars each in a huge range of ratios (30:1 up to 1000:1). These are the cheapest way to get a drive motor and are extremely popular in plastic antweights, but they carry a well-known "quality lottery" risk: two motors from the same listing, or even the same order, can have different windings, different gear materials (mixed metal/plastic despite "metal gear" branding), and different failure rates. Pololu's micro metal gearmotors (HP/MP/LP power tunes, many ratio options) sit between these tiers: a precision-manufactured 10x12mm metal gearbox with well-documented, consistent specs, at a price above generic N-series but usually below FingerTech/Repeat.

Finally, modified or stock continuous-rotation 9g servos offer the simplest possible drive for plastic antweights: no ESC required, direct receiver connection, at the cost of speed and torque. Choosing among these options is mostly a question of budget, desired reliability, and how much of the 454g weight budget is left after the weapon, battery, and armor.
