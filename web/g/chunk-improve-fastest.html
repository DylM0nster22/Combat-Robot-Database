How to improve fastest as a new builder

Getting started

antweightplastic-antweightgetting-startedstrategy

An honest ordering of what actually makes you better, from highest return to lowest.

1. Go to an event, even without a robot. One day of watching the pits teaches more than a month of reading. You will see what breaks, how people fix it, and how much of the sport is driving.

2. Finish a simple robot and fight it. A control bot or a beater bar that survives every match teaches you more than an ambitious spinner that fails inspection. Completion beats sophistication for the first build.

3. Drive deliberately. Twenty minutes a week of structured practice — the opening three seconds, inverted driving, angled approaches, one side dead — is worth more than any component upgrade.

4. Fix the thing that broke, properly. After each event, find the actual root cause rather than the symptom. A wheel that fell off twice is a retention design problem, not a tightening problem.

5. Copy a good design and understand why it works. The antweight community shares designs freely. Building a proven design teaches you the reasoning; the mistake is copying without understanding, because you cannot then repair or adapt it.

6. Learn the maths for the decisions that matter. You do not need all of it. You need: tip speed and kinetic energy (is my weapon worth anything), spin-up time (will I be box-rushed), traction limit (is more gearing pointless), and the weight budget (what am I actually spending grams on).

7. Then optimise components. Better motors and better materials are real but they are the last 10%, and they only pay off once the robot reliably finishes matches.

The single most common failure mode for new builders is inverting this list: starting with an ambitious design and expensive parts, never finishing it, and never getting the driving practice that would have mattered most.
