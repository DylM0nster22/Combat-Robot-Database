The Full Combat Robotics Weight Class Ladder

Combat robotics weight classes form a ladder from a few hundred grams up to a couple hundred pounds, and almost every rule in the sport (armor thickness, weapon energy, safety inspection strictness, arena construction) scales with which rung a robot sits on. From smallest to largest, the classes most builders encounter are:

For a beginner, the practical takeaway is that antweight (1 lb) and its plastic-only sibling, plastic antweight, sit at the bottom of the US ladder for a reason: they are the cheapest and safest classes to build and fight in, they are run at nearly every event on the calendar, and they teach the same design, wiring, and driving skills that scale up to every heavier class later. The UK runs an entirely separate, much lighter ladder starting at 150g 'Antweight' (see the fairyweight/antweight naming-confusion chunk), so a US builder reading a UK rulebook, or vice versa, needs to check units and grams/pounds carefully before assuming a class name means the same thing.

No single number is 'the' heavyweight limit: BattleBots' TV division is 250 lb, RoboGames also runs 250 lb (with a 500 lb walking-bonus tier), and older/traditional SPARC heavyweight rules cap at 220 lb. Always confirm the exact number against the specific event's current rulebook rather than assuming cross-organization consistency, especially at the top of the ladder where the difference between 220 lb and 250 lb is a real, non-trivial design constraint.
