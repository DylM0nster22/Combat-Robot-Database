SPARC: The Unified US Combat Robotics Standard

Major Rulesets

SPARC (Standardized Procedures for the Advancement of Robotic Combat) exists to solve a coordination problem: before it, every regional US combat robotics event wrote its own rulebook, and a robot legal at one event could be illegal at the next over some tiny weight or safety difference. SPARC is a community-maintained rules standard (hosted at sparc.tools) that most active US antweight/beetleweight/hobbyweight-and-up events now adopt wholesale or with only minor local additions, so a 'SPARC-legal' robot travels between events without a rebuild.

SPARC's weight ladder runs Fairyweight (150 g), Antweight (1 lb), Beetleweight (3 lb), Mantisweight (6 lb), Hobbyweight (12 lb), Dogeweight (15 lb), Featherweight (30 lb), Lightweight (60 lb), Middleweight (120 lb), and Heavyweight (220 lb), each with its own safety and construction requirements scaled to the destructive potential of that class. On top of the base ladder, SPARC layers several important variant rules: the plastic-class rules (Plastic Antweight, Plastic Beetleweight) restricting construction to a defined list of 3D-printable plastics; the walker/shuffler weight-bonus rules (100% for true walkers, up to 50% for shufflers); and multibot/clusterbot rules governing how weight is shared and bonused across multiple physically separate robots fighting as one entry.

Safety-wise, SPARC-aligned events converge on the same core requirements found across nearly every other ruleset in this space: a tool-free external main power disconnect, a radio system with a fail-safe that halts all motion on signal loss, a visible weapon lock fitted whenever the robot is powered outside the arena, and a banned-weapons list excluding entanglement devices, liquid/fluid weapons, unlicensed flame weapons, explosives, and RF-jamming/EMP weapons. Because SPARC is community-maintained rather than owned by a single company, exact wording and edge-case rulings can evolve between events and years, so competitors are expected to check the current version at sparc.tools rather than relying on an older cached copy — a real risk when researching the rules from an old PDF or forum thread, since the version referenced by many public sources is the older v1.2 document rather than the latest text.
