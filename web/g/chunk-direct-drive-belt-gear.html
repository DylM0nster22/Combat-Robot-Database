Direct drive vs belt vs gear reduction for weapon drives

Drivetrain Layout

antweightplastic-antweightbelt-drivedirect-drivegear-ratiohtdgt2

Direct drive bolts the weapon (or its hub) straight onto the motor shaft or bell with a 1:1 ratio. It is mechanically simplest -- no belts, pulleys, or reduction to design -- and is the default for most 1 lb vertical disc/bar spinners using 18xx-22xx motors, especially purpose-built hubmotors like the Repeat 2207 that are designed for the weapon disc to bolt directly to the bell. Its downside is that the motor's shaft and bearings absorb every impact shock directly, and the motor's KV must already be close to the exact value needed for your target tip speed at your chosen voltage, since there is no ratio to tune it with.

Belt drive (almost always GT2 or HTD 3mm pitch at antweight scale) puts a small pulley on the motor and a larger pulley on the weapon shaft (or dead shaft hub), connected by a toothed timing belt. This buys three things: (1) it isolates the motor's shaft and bearings from the worst of the weapon impact shock, since the belt has some compliance and the weapon load path can be routed through a separate, more robust weapon shaft and bearings instead of the motor's own; (2) it lets you use a motor whose natural KV doesn't match your target tip speed 1:1 -- you gear it down (or up) with the pulley ratio instead of hunting for an exact-KV motor; (3) it lets you mount the motor somewhere more protected in the frame rather than directly behind the weapon. Typical antweight belt ratios run from about 1.5:1 to 3:1 (weapon pulley teeth divided by motor pulley teeth), with something like a 10-tooth motor pulley and an 18-20 tooth weapon pulley being a common starting point for a roughly 2:1 reduction. Reduction ratio also multiplies torque at the weapon shaft (minus belt/bearing losses) while dividing RPM, so a 2:1 belt reduction on a high-torque, moderate-KV motor is a common way to get both good bite and comfortable spin-up time.

Gear reduction (spur gears rather than a belt) is much less common at antweight scale -- it is heavier, less forgiving of misalignment, and offers no shock-isolation benefit over a belt, so most antweight builders who want a reduction reach for a timing belt instead. Gearboxes see more use in drive trains than in weapon drives at this weight class.

Dead-shaft belt mounting is the standard way to combine a belt reduction with easy pit maintenance: the weapon shaft itself is bolted rigidly to the frame and does not rotate, while the weapon hub (blade + pulley, bolted together) spins on bearings around that fixed shaft. This makes swapping a stretched or damaged belt during a competition much faster than removing a rotating, keyed shaft and its retaining hardware -- you simply lift the hub/pulley/blade assembly off the fixed shaft.
