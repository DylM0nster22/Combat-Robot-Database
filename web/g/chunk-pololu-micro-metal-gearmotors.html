Pololu Micro Metal Gearmotors: HP, MP, LP and Extended-Shaft Variants

Drive Motors

antweightplastic-antweightdrive-motorpololumicro-metal-gearmotorhp-mp-lp

Pololu's micro metal gearmotor line sits in a useful middle ground between the quality-lottery risk of generic N-series motors and the higher price of FingerTech/Repeat combat-specific products: a precision-manufactured, well-documented 10x12mm all-metal gearbox on a 3mm D-shaped output shaft (9mm long), sold in a wide range of gear ratios and, distinctively, in three separate power tunes of the same base motor.

The three tunes — LP (Low Power), MP (Medium Power), and HP (High Power) — use the same physical gearbox and shaft but different motor winding/brush choices, trading current draw for speed and torque at any given ratio. At 6V, LP draws a stall current of about 0.36A (free-run ~40mA), MP about 0.67A (free-run ~70mA), and HP about 1.6A (free-run ~100mA). All three use shorter-life precious-metal brushes at 6V, which are lower-friction and preferred for these lower-current applications; Pololu also sells higher-voltage (12V) versions of some ratios with carbon brushes better suited to sustained higher-current use. For antweight drive, HP is the tune most builders reach for when they want the most power in the smallest package, while LP or MP make sense when a design is current-budget constrained, for example sharing a small ESC or battery branch with other electronics.

A useful variant is the extended motor shaft option, which brings the motor's rear shaft out through the back of the case in addition to the normal gearbox output shaft. This lets a builder mount a wheel-speed encoder disc, a second small pulley, or another accessory on the back of the motor without any machining — handy for a control-bot build that wants closed-loop wheel-speed feedback, or a 4WD layout that needs to drive an extra belt/pulley off the same motor.

The main trade-off versus FingerTech's Spark line is shaft length and mounting convention: Pololu's 9mm output shaft is too short for FingerTech's typical direct-wheel-mount (live-axle) approach and needs a hub or coupler, and Pololu doesn't publish a combat-specific mounting pattern the way FingerTech does. In exchange, a builder gets Pololu's much larger and more precisely documented range of ratios and power tunes, plus the reliability of a company whose entire business is measuring and publishing exactly this kind of spec — a meaningful advantage over generic N-series sourcing for a builder who wants predictable performance without over-paying for a combat-specific brand name.
