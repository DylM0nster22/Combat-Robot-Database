What drivetrain each archetype needs

Design

antweightplastic-antweightdrivetraindesign

The archetype sets the drivetrain requirement, and getting this wrong wastes more weight than any other single decision.

Spinners need enough drive, not maximum drive. A spinner wins by landing hits, which needs controllable positioning rather than raw pushing force. Two brushed 16 mm gearmotors at a moderate ratio, giving roughly 2-4 m/s, is the standard. Spending more weight on drive takes it from the weapon, which is where a spinner's value is.

Control bots and wedges need traction above all. Pushing force is limited by friction, not torque: max push = coefficient of friction × weight on driven wheels. For a 454 g robot with grippy tyres that is only around 4-5 N. Once you have enough torque to reach that limit, more gearing achieves nothing — the wheels just slip. So the priorities are soft tyres, weight over the driven wheels, and four-wheel drive to put all 454 g to work.

Lifters, flippers and grabbers need torque and control. They must hold position while the mechanism works, which means low-speed controllability matters more than top speed. Higher gear ratios, and a drivetrain that does not stall when pushed.

Thwackbots need rotational acceleration. Their weapon energy comes from how fast the drivetrain can spin the whole robot, so they want high torque and high-grip tyres at a wide track.

Melty brains need matched, predictable motors. The translation algorithm assumes both sides respond identically. Mismatched motors make the robot curve.

A general antweight rule: wheel diameter sets your speed for a given rpm, and it is usually easier to change speed by changing wheels than by changing gearboxes. A 500 rpm output on a 32 mm wheel gives about 0.84 m/s; the same motor on a 50 mm wheel gives 1.3 m/s with proportionally less torque at the contact patch.
