Why the wedge-plus-spinner hybrid dominates

Meta

antweightmetastrategy

Modern competitive antweight converges on one configuration: a vertical spinner or drum with low hinged wedgelets flanking the weapon. The reason is a rock-paper-scissors that does not quite close.

Start with the three pure archetypes:

  • Vertical spinners beat control bots. Upward damage is unanswerable by a robot with no weapon.
  • Wedges beat horizontal spinners. The bar sweeps over a floor-contacting edge while the wedge lifts them.
  • Horizontal spinners beat vertical spinners. Reach and sweep attack the vertical's exposed weapon and wheels.

If that were the whole picture the class would cycle. It does not, because of one asymmetry: a wedge can be bolted onto a spinner, but a spinner cannot be bolted onto a wedge. Wedgelets cost 20-30 grams and no weapon-system complexity. A weapon costs 30% of the weight budget plus an ESC plus battery load.

So the stable equilibrium is the archetype that carries both: it wins the ground game against wedges and control bots, and it carries enough damage to beat them, while still having a weapon for anything else.

What actually counters it. Two things:

  • The undercutter, which strikes below the wedgelet line. This is the principled counter — it attacks exactly the component that makes the hybrid strong. The reason it has not taken over is that running a bar a few millimetres off the floor, and keeping it there after impacts, is genuinely difficult.
  • The grabber, which neutralises any weapon once clamped. The reason it has not taken over is that closing on a live weapon with jaws open rarely works.

Both counters are hard to build. That is why the hybrid is stable rather than just currently popular. If someone solves undercutter ground-clearance robustness at 1 lb, the meta moves.
