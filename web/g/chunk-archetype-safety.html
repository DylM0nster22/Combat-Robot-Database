Archetype-specific safety

Safety

antweightplastic-antweightsafetyrules

Different archetypes present different hazards, and the safety requirements follow the archetype.

Spinners store the most energy and are the most dangerous. A competitive 1 lb spinner can hold a few hundred joules — enough to break bone or destroy an eye. The rules that exist because of spinners:

  • A weapon lock — a physical device that prevents the weapon rotating — must be fitted whenever the robot is powered outside the arena, and removed only inside it.
  • A removable link or accessible power switch so the robot can be made safe without tools.
  • A tested failsafe that stops the weapon if the radio signal is lost. This is the single most-checked item at tech inspection.

Flippers, springs and pressure systems are dangerous when stored energy is released unexpectedly. A cocked spring is a loaded weapon. Never work on a cocked mechanism; discharge it first. Pressurised systems attract additional scrutiny and are restricted or banned at some events.

Thwackbots and melty brains are unusual in that the whole robot is moving unpredictably. They are hard for a referee to make safe and hard for a driver to stop precisely.

Control bots and wedges are the safest archetype to build and test, which is another argument for building one first.

Universal rules regardless of archetype: never spin a weapon outside a proper enclosure, treat the robot as live whenever the battery is connected, and set the failsafe before the first time you power up — not the night before the event.
