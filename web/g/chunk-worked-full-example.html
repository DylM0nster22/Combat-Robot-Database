Worked example: full weapon-motor sizing from target to selection

Worked Examples

antweightworked-examplesizing-walkthrough

This walks through a complete antweight weapon-motor sizing exercise end to end, chaining every formula in this file together, to show how the numbers actually connect in practice.

Goal: a 1 lb antweight direct-drive vertical disc, 4 inch (101.6mm) diameter, 150g weapon mass, targeting a competitive but not extreme 230 mph tip speed on a 4S pack (16.8V fully charged).

Step 1 -- target RPM. Using the tip-speed formula rearranged: RPM = v_mph 336.14 / D_in = 230 336.14 / 4 = 19,328 RPM.

Step 2 -- required KV. Direct drive means ratio = 1.0: KV_needed = RPM / V_pack = 19328 / 16.8 = 1151 kv. This lands squarely in the common 22xx-class KV range, so a 2205, 2206, or 2207 wound around 1100-1200kv is the natural choice; pick the 2207 for extra stator volume and torque margin.

Step 3 -- torque constant. Kt = 8.27 / 1151 = 0.00719 Nm/A. At a realistic sustained/near-stall current of 30A for this class of motor, available torque approx 0.00719 * 30 = 0.216 Nm.

Step 4 -- weapon MOI. Approximating the 150g (0.15 kg) disc as a uniform solid disc of radius 0.0508m: I = 0.5 m r^2 = 0.5 0.15 0.0508^2 = 0.0001935 kg*m^2.

Step 5 -- stored kinetic energy at full speed. omega = RPM pi/30 = 19328 pi/30 = 2024 rad/s. KE = 0.5 I omega^2 = 0.5 0.0001935 2024^2 = 0.5 0.0001935 4,096,600 approx 396 J -- a solid, competitive hit for a 1 lb vertical disc.

Step 6 -- spin-up time estimate. Average accelerating torque approx half of stall torque: tau_avg approx 0.5 0.216 = 0.108 Nm. t = (I omega) / tau_avg = (0.0001935 * 2024) / 0.108 = 0.3917 / 0.108 approx 3.6 s.

Interpretation. A 3.6 second full spin-up is on the slower side of acceptable for a 3-minute antweight match -- workable, but a builder who wants faster recovery after a stall might trade some tip speed (say, dropping the target to 200 mph, which lowers RPM, KE, and spin-up time together) or move to a slightly larger-stator 2207/2306 winding at the same KV for more absolute torque without changing the speed target at all. This is exactly the kind of trade-off the size ladder and KV-selection formulas exist to make explicit rather than left to guesswork.
