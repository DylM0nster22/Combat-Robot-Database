What Defines the Plastic Antweight Class

Class Definition

plastic-antweightrulesdefinitionmaterial-restriction

Plastic antweight (also called "plastic ant," "PLA ant," or "3D printed antweight") is a 1 lb / 454 g combat robot class where the weight limit is identical to a normal antweight, but the construction material is restricted: the chassis, armor and weapon(s) must be built only from a short list of 3D-printable plastics. Under the SPARC Robot Construction Specifications (v1.4, section 4.4), which most North American events either quote directly or lightly modify, the allowed list is PET, PETG, ABS, PLA, or PLA+, with SCAR's ruleset also explicitly naming ASA. No other plastic or material is legal for structural/armor/weapon use unless an event organizer grants an exception 'that meets the spirit of the rules.'

Crucially, the rule targets structure, armor, and weapons — not every part of the robot. A wide range of non-plastic components are explicitly carved out as exempt: motors, electronics, axles, fasteners, and adhesives can be any material, as long as they are not used 'in such a way to enhance the structural integrity, armor the robot, or enhance any weapon' (SPARC 4.4.2, echoed nearly verbatim by SCAR and Robot Ruckus). In practice this means a plastic ant's motors, its steel weapon shaft, its bearings, and its screws can all be metal — only the printed shell, weapon disc/drum body, and any load-bearing armor panels must be plastic.

Most events also require that the plastic be 3D printed specifically, not just any plastic sheet or rod stock cut or machined into shape. This closes an obvious loophole: a CNC-milled or laser-cut solid polycarbonate/UHMW part would be far stronger than an FDM-printed part of the same material and geometry, defeating the class's purpose of keeping material strength low. The RobotCombatWiki entry on the class states this plainly: 'many if not most events will require plastic parts to be only 3D printed' specifically to prevent this kind of milled-part loophole.

The class runs as its own bracket — plastic ants never fight standard-material antweights in the same pool — because the material restriction is understood to make plastic ants meaningfully weaker/more fragile fighters than a metal antweight of the same weight, not merely a cosmetic build constraint.
