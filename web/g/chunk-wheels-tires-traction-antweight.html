Wheels and Tires: Foam, Silicone, TPU, O-Rings, and Banebots

Wheels

Wheel choice on an antweight is a direct trade between weight, grip, shock absorption, and cost, and the options span a wide range of approaches.

FingerTech Lite Flite foam wheels are the default choice for most standard-material antweights: light neoprene foam tires available in diameters from 25.4mm to 76.2mm (1.0-3.0in) in 6.35mm (0.25in) steps, in two widths (12.7mm/0.5in and 19.1mm/0.75in), mounted via FingerTech's Lite, Snap, or Twist hub system. A 2.0in tire alone weighs only about 4g, and the foam's compliance absorbs shock from arena hazards and hard impacts that would otherwise transmit straight into the gearbox and shaft. Grip is described as "surprisingly good" for how light the material is, but it's still a step below purpose-built rubber compounds.

Silicone-coated or cast-silicone tires address that grip gap directly: a thin layer of 2-part silicone rubber, typically in the Shore A 30-50 durometer range, cast or brushed onto a foam or printed core. Softer compounds in that range grip best on polished wood or acrylic/polycarbonate arena floors, at the cost of faster wear and more debris pickup; it's a common DIY upgrade for control bots that need to win pushing matches rather than just drive around.

3D-printed TPU wheels, printed in flexible filament typically rated 85-95A Shore hardness, are the natural fit for plastic antweights: essentially free once a printer and filament are on hand, printable as a single piece with the hub bore built in (eliminating a separate hub's weight), and highly impact-resistant. Grip sits above rigid PLA/PETG wheels but below a soft cast-silicone tire at similar hardness.

O-ring tires are a cheap, DIY-friendly technique across weight classes: a plain hub (aluminum, printed, or Delrin) machined or printed with a groove sized for a rubber O-ring, stretched on as the tread. They're inexpensive to replace when worn, give solid rubber-compound grip, and let the hub itself stay very light — the main risk is the O-ring rolling in its groove under high torque if the groove isn't sized tightly enough.

Banebots wheels represent the name-brand compliant-tread option: the T61 line offers 6 hardness options (from a very soft, maximum-grip 10A yellow rubber up to a firmer, more durable 60A black rubber), 4 diameters, and 4 hub shaft sizes, while the smaller T40 line is aimed more specifically at ant/fairy-scale builds. Banebots wheels are heavier than foam or TPU for a given diameter — a real cost against a 1 lb weight budget — but their compliant tread conforms to the floor for grip and control that's hard to match with a rigid or foam alternative, which is why they show up more often on control-bot and pushing-focused designs than on speed- or weight-optimized spinners.

Across all of these, the general rule holds: softer/more compliant tread gives more grip but wears faster and adds weight/debris-pickup; harder/lighter tread (foam, rigid TPU) saves weight and lasts longer but gives up some pushing power to wheel slip on a smooth arena floor.
