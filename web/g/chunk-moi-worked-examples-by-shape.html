MOI worked examples across weapon shapes: disc, ring, bar, point mass, drum

MOI and Energy

antweightplastic-antweightmoiworked-examplediscringbardrum

Moment of inertia (MOI) is the single number that most determines how a weapon of a given mass and radius will behave -- how much energy it stores at a given rpm, and how long it takes to spin up. The design-math topic covers the base derivations (formula-moi-solid-disc, formula-moi-annulus, formula-moi-thin-ring, formula-moi-slender-rod-center, formula-moi-point-mass, formula-moi-thin-walled-cylinder); this section applies each shape to a realistic 1 lb weapon geometry side by side, so the practical differences between weapon forms are visible in concrete numbers rather than abstract formulas.

Solid disc (76.2 mm / 3in diameter, 227 g steel disc, R=0.0381m): I = 0.5mR^2 = 0.50.2270.0381^2 = 1.65e-4 kg*m^2.

Thin ring / shell spinner (same 227 g mass, but as a thin ring at the same 38.1 mm radius rather than a solid disc): I = mR^2 = 0.2270.0381^2 = 3.30e-4 kg*m^2 -- exactly double the solid disc's MOI for the identical mass and radius, the numeric heart of why shell spinners hit so hard for their weight (see formula-wg-mass-toward-rim-effect).

Annulus / partial rim-loading (227 g disc reshaped into a ring with R_out=38.1mm, R_in=28.6mm, i.e. a moderately thick rim rather than a knife-thin one): I = 0.5m(R_out^2+R_in^2) = 0.50.227(0.001452+0.000818) = 0.50.2270.00227 = 2.58e-4 kg*m^2 -- roughly 56% more than the solid disc, and a realistic middle ground between a plain disc and a full thin-ring shell.

Slender bar about its center (70 mm long, 60 g bar): I = (1/12)mL^2 = (1/12)0.0600.070^2 = 2.45e-5 kg*m^2 -- notice this is much smaller than the disc examples above because both the bar's mass and its tip radius (35 mm vs ~38 mm) are considerably smaller in this example; MOI comparisons only mean something when comparing weapons of similar total mass and radius.

Point mass at radius (a single 15 g bolt-on tooth at 33 mm radius, ignoring the hub it's attached to for illustration): I = mr^2 = 0.0150.033^2 = 1.63e-5 kg*m^2. In a real weapon this tooth's contribution is added to the hub/body's own MOI via the parallel axis theorem (formula-parallel-axis-theorem), not used alone.

Thin-walled cylinder / drum (100 g drum, 40 mm OD, 3 mm wall, R_out=0.020m, R_in=0.017m): I = 0.5m(R_out^2+R_in^2) = 0.50.100(0.0004+0.000289) = 3.445e-5 kg*m^2 -- see formula-wg-drum-wall-thickness-moi for how this changes with wall thickness at the same OD and mass.

Takeaway. For a given total weapon mass and outer radius, MOI (and therefore stored energy at a given rpm) ranks from lowest to highest roughly as: solid disc < moderate annulus < thin ring/shell, with drums and bars falling wherever their specific mass and radius put them on that same disc-to-ring spectrum. This is why 'how is the mass distributed relative to the axis' is a more useful first question when comparing two weapon designs than diameter or weight alone.
