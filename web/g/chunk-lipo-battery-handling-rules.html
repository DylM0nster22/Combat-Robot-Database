LiPo Battery Handling, Charging, and Arena Requirements

Safety & Inspection

fairyweightantweightplastic-antweightbeetleweightlipobattery-safetyfire-riskpit-safetyarena

Lithium-polymer (LiPo) batteries are the dominant power source across nearly every combat robot weight class, from plastic antweight up through TV-scale heavyweights, because of their excellent power-to-weight ratio — but that same energy density makes them the single most common fire-safety concern at any event, and pit safety rules around LiPo handling are taken very seriously after real incidents, including LiPo fires in the pit area at RoboGames 2017 that led directly to fireproof battery bags becoming mandatory rather than optional at many events afterward.

The near-universal practices that show up across event safety documents are: charge only inside a fireproof LiPo charging bag or a vented metal/ammo-can-style box, never inside a plastic tote, cardboard box, or bare on a table; never leave a battery charging unattended, even briefly — an unattended charging LiPo is treated as a serious pit-safety violation that can get a competitor removed from the event; inspect every battery before use for physical damage (punctures, cuts to the outer wrap, bent or corroded connectors) and especially for swelling/puffing, which indicates internal damage and means the cell should be retired immediately rather than risked in a fight; and never allow a battery to be crushed, punctured, or have its leads crossed/shorted, since any of these can trigger thermal runaway — the battery catching fire or venting violently.

Beyond individual battery handling, arena and venue-level requirements exist specifically because of LiPo risk: venues are commonly expected to be ventilated for roughly one full air exchange every 60 seconds, and any area where LiPo batteries are present (pits, charging stations, staging areas) should have a clear, obstruction-free evacuation route to open air, since a LiPo fire can produce toxic smoke quickly. AWS's rules add an antweight-specific voltage cap of 24V, and require that batteries not contain liquid acid or free electrolyte — ruling out old-style wet-cell chemistries entirely, though this is now largely a non-issue given LiPo and LiFe's dominance at every scale from fairyweight up.

For an antweight or plastic-antweight builder specifically, the practical rules of thumb that follow directly from event safety requirements are: buy a small fireproof LiPo bag (they are inexpensive and reusable across a builder's entire career), always charge in that bag on a non-flammable surface away from anything combustible, never charge overnight or walk away mid-charge, retire any cell that develops even mild puffing rather than trying to "get one more fight" out of it, and store spare batteries at storage voltage (roughly 3.8V/cell) rather than full charge when not competing soon, since long-term storage at full charge accelerates degradation and swelling risk.
