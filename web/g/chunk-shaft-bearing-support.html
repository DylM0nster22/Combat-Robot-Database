Shaft support and bearing selection for antweight weapons

Attachment and Failure

antweightplastic-antweightshaftbearingsdead-shaftlive-shaft

A weapon shaft's job is to hold the weapon perfectly centered and running true under the most violent loads anywhere on the robot, and the choices here -- single vs double bearing support, dead vs live shaft, and bearing type -- interact closely with each other.

Single vs double bearing support. A shaft supported by only one bearing, with the weapon mounted as a cantilever off the end, sees bending stress that scales directly with the unsupported length between the bearing and the load, as shown in formula-wg-cantilever-shaft-bending: a 6 mm shaft under an estimated 1000 N impact spike with a 10 mm unsupported span reaches roughly 472 MPa of bending stress (enough to permanently bend a mild-steel shaft), while shortening that span to 3 mm with a second, closer support point drops the stress to about 141 MPa. Wherever the weapon geometry allows it, supporting the shaft on both sides of the weapon's mass (or as close to both sides as the design permits) is structurally far superior to a single-bearing cantilever, particularly for wider weapons like drums where the unsupported span would otherwise be large.

Dead shaft vs live shaft. A dead shaft (term-dead-shaft) bolts rigidly to the frame and never rotates; the weapon spins around it on its own bearings, which conveniently lets those bearings sit right inside the weapon's mass rather than needing a rotating shaft supported far apart at the frame rails -- a common and effective way to get good bearing support on a compact antweight disc or drum. A live shaft (term-live-shaft) rotates in frame-mounted bearings with the weapon keyed/pinned/set-screwed directly onto it; simpler for a single small disc close to the frame, but for wider weapons it needs support bearings positioned close to both ends of the weapon or it behaves as a cantilever, per the bending-stress relationship above.

Bearings for ants. The most common ball bearing sizes at antweight scale are 623 (3x10x4mm, component-bearing-623), 626 (6x19x6mm, component-bearing-626), and 688 (8x16x5mm, component-bearing-688), with flanged variants (component-bearing-flanged) preferred in dead-shaft weapon bells where the bearing needs to self-locate axially in a bore without extra retention hardware. Bronze bushings (component-bronze-bushing) are a lighter-duty plain-bearing alternative: cheaper and more tolerant of dirt and minor misalignment, at the cost of higher friction and a lower safe rpm ceiling, making them a better fit for slower drivetrain or low-speed weapon applications than for a full-speed disc or shell spinner. Bearing choice also constrains shaft diameter directly (a larger bore supports a stiffer, stronger shaft), so bearing and shaft sizing should be worked out together rather than picking a shaft size first and finding a bearing to fit it afterward.
