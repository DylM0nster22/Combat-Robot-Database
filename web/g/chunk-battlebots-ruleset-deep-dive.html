BattleBots: The TV-Scale Heavyweight Standard

Major Rulesets

heavyweightbattlebotsrulesetheavyweighttv-league

BattleBots is the most visible combat robotics competition to the general public, run as a televised series with a single professional-tier weight class: 250 lb (113 kg) ready-to-fight, with an allowance up to 500 lb for a robot that qualifies as a true walker using articulated legs and meets additional design criteria BattleBots specifies. This mirrors the same 100%-bonus-for-true-walkers pattern used across essentially every other ruleset in the sport, just at a far larger absolute mass than hobby classes.

One distinctive BattleBots requirement is a functional test for lifting/grappling weapons: any robot entering with a lifter, flipper, or grappling weapon must demonstrate, as part of pre-event inspection, that the weapon can actually lift 250 lb to a height of 12 inches. This is a meaningful bar — it means a flipper can't just look impressive on paper or in CAD, it has to prove it can flip a full-weight opponent in a live functional test before being cleared to compete, which keeps underpowered 'flippers' from entering the arena only to fail on air.

Every robot goes through BattleBots' full in-person technical and safety inspection, commonly referred to as 'the box,' before being cleared to fight — a far more rigorous process than most hobby-scale events can afford to run, reflecting both the higher stakes (250 lb robots at full power are genuinely dangerous) and BattleBots' larger operating budget and professional crew.

When a match runs the full duration without a knockout, BattleBots' published Judges' Guide (recent seasons) uses an 11-point scale per judge across a 3-judge panel: 5 points for Damage, 3 points for Aggression, and 3 points for Control. Damage is scored on the relative amount of non-cosmetic damage dealt to each robot — including self-inflicted damage and any system that stops working correctly by the end of the match, whether or not it looks visually dramatic. Aggression is scored on the frequency, severity, boldness, and intent of attacks a robot deliberately initiates against its opponent, rewarding a robot that presses the fight rather than one that merely survives. Control is scored on how well and how long a robot dictates the terms of the fight — pushing the opponent around, lifting it, pinning it, or using arena hazards/walls against it. This point split is not permanent: ahead of the BattleBots Pro League in 2026, the judging system was revised to merge Aggression with a new 'Engagement' concept into a single 5-point scored category, so competitors and analysts should always check the current season's official Judges' Guide rather than assume any one year's exact split carries forward. What has stayed constant across seasons is the baseline mechanical requirement: 250 lb standard weight, 500 lb walker allowance, and the mandatory 250 lb-to-12-inch lift test for any lifting/grappling weapon system.
