Recommended Drivetrain: Competitive 1 lb Antweight Spinner

Recommendations

antweightrecommendationspinnerdrivetrainantweight

A competitive antweight spinner spends most of its weight budget on the weapon system (motor, bar/disc, containment) and armor, so the drivetrain's job is to be light, reliable, and "just fast/agile enough" to line up hits and dodge — not to win pushing matches. Recommended baseline:

  • Topology: 2WD direct (live-axle) drive. A spinner rarely needs 4WD's extra traction, and every gram saved on the drivetrain can go into the weapon or armor instead.
  • Motors: A pair of FingerTech Silver Spark gearmotors in the 22.2:1-33.3:1 range are the proven default — enough torque margin over the traction limit for confident repositioning, with a reasonable top speed (roughly 4-6 mph depending on wheel size) for closing distance and dodging counter-attacks. Builders chasing extra speed and efficiency at similar weight increasingly use a Repeat Robotics brushless "Mini" 1106 drive pod instead, which can be tuned faster on 3S while staying in the same weight class as a Silver Spark pair.
  • Wheels: 1.25-1.5in FingerTech Lite Flite foam wheels, prioritizing shock absorption (protecting the drivetrain from the vibration and impacts of being near an active spinning weapon and from opposing hits) over maximum grip — a spinner's own gyroscopic effects and hit-and-retreat playstyle rely less on sustained high-friction pushing than a control bot does.
  • Ground clearance and wheel guards: Keep wheels tucked fully inside the body's outer footprint, ideally behind a hardened skirt or armor lip, since exposed wheels are one of the most common ways a spinner robot gets killed — a single solid hit to an unguarded wheel can end the match instantly.
  • Battery/voltage: 2S LiPo (7.4V) is the standard baseline; 3S is worth considering specifically to unlock a brushless drive pod's higher speed range, but adds weight that must be budgeted against the (typically much heavier) weapon system.

The overarching principle for a spinner's drivetrain is: size it to comfortably clear the traction limit with margin (per the traction-limit formula) at a ratio that gives useful positioning speed, then stop — any drivetrain weight or complexity beyond that is weight the weapon system could have used instead.
