Worked Example: Sizing a Battery for a New Antweight Spinner

Worked Example

antweightplastic-antweightworked-examplepower-budgetsizing

Putting the formulas in this file together for a concrete antweight build: a 1 lb horizontal spinner with two small brushed drive motors and a 3S-rated brushless weapon motor around 2200KV.

Step 1 — estimate current draw. Drive: two brushed motors at roughly 2A typical each under normal driving = 4A combined, with stall spikes to perhaps 12-16A if both jam simultaneously (formula-power-budget-drive-weapon). Weapon: the 2200KV motor on 3S (11.1V nominal) draws roughly 6-9A once spun up and cutting normally, with brief spikes above 15A at spin-up or on a hard hit to an opponent. Combined typical draw: roughly 10-13A; combined worst-case brief spike: roughly 25-30A.

Step 2 — set continuous and burst current targets. Applying a 1.5-2x safety margin to the 10-13A typical figure gives a target continuous rating of roughly 20-25A; the worst-case spike figure of ~30A sets the burst target.

Step 3 — pick a candidate pack and discount its C rating. A CNHL MiniStar 3S 450mAh 70C pack claims (0.45Ah x 70) = 31.5A continuous on paper (formula-continuous-current-c-rating). Applying the realistic 40-60% discount for planning gives roughly 13-19A of trustworthy continuous capability — this falls a bit short of the 20-25A target computed in Step 2, suggesting either stepping up to a higher-rated pack (a genuine 100C-class pack from a reputable brand) or a larger-capacity pack at the same C rating (a 550mAh pack at 70C nominally claims 38.5A, discounting to roughly 15-23A) to close the gap with more margin.

Step 4 — check runtime. Using the 450mAh pack at 80% usable capacity (360mAh) against the ~10-13A typical combined draw: Runtime = (360/11500) x 60 ≈ 1.9 minutes at continuous full-typical-load — noticeably short of a 3-minute match if the bot ran flat-out the whole time, but real matches include lulls, positioning, and pauses that bring average draw well below the "flat-out typical" figure used here, which is exactly why this worked example, like the real product landscape, points toward sizing up: a 550mAh (or a pair of 450mAh packs to swap between preliminary rounds) gives meaningfully more margin on both the current-rating shortfall from Step 3 and the runtime tightness from Step 4, for a a few extra grams of pack weight — a trade almost every antweight builder makes deliberately rather than by accident.

Step 5 — sanity-check voltage sag. Even a healthy 3S pack with 0.03 ohm/cell (0.09 ohm total) hit with a 25A worst-case spike sags roughly 2.25V (formula-voltage-sag-estimate), from 11.1V down to about 8.85V for that instant — comfortably above most 3S-rated ESC/receiver brownout thresholds when the pack is fresh, but a real warning that an aged or previously-hit pack with double that internal resistance would sag to a genuinely risky ~6.6V under the same spike, reinforcing why pack age and impact history matter as much as the printed spec sheet.
