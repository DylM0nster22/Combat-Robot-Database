Where the Plastic Ant Community Shares Designs

Community

plastic-antweightcommunityprintablesthingiverseopen-sourceresources

Plastic antweight has one of the most open, actively-shared design cultures in combat robotics, which is itself part of why the class is such a strong entry point — a beginner rarely has to design a robot completely from scratch.

Printables (printables.com, Prusa's model-sharing platform) hosts several named open-source plastic ant designs, including Force Knight, a free 1lb plastic antweight by community designer Michael K described as intended for SCAR-legal component kits, and a community-shared 3D-printed drum spinner design demonstrating the drum-spinner-as-durability-choice pattern discussed throughout this file.

Thingiverse hosts additional well-known open designs, including the CRNZ Antweight Combat Robot Kit by CombatRoboticsNZ — an explicitly modular, extensible printable chassis meant to be a base other builders customize and upgrade rather than a fixed one-off design — and, on the UK 150g side of the naming divide, Legally Distinct, a drum spinner by community designer Blessy openly credited as inspired by the established UK antweight Siren.

Cults3D also lists purchasable/downloadable plastic ant model files (e.g., a specifically-tagged 'Plastic 1lb Antweight Vertical Spinner' design), functioning similarly to Printables/Thingiverse but with more paid listings mixed among free ones.

Onshape and other cloud CAD tools are widely used for public, shareable combat-robot CAD documents in the broader combat robotics community (a pattern well established in metal antweight/beetleweight circles), letting other builders view, fork, and remix a design's actual parametric CAD rather than just a fixed STL — though specific named public plastic-ant Onshape documents were not independently confirmed in the sources reviewed for this file (confidence: low on Onshape specifically, though the practice is well-established in the wider community).

Discord servers run by regional combat robotics clubs and larger events (SCAR, Robot Ruckus, and similar organizations typically maintain one) are the community's primary place for real-time build questions, rules clarifications ('is X material legal at your event'), and informal design-sharing outside the more formal model-repository sites — a pattern consistent with how modern hobbyist engineering communities generally organize online, though this file was not able to confirm specific named plastic-ant Discord servers independently (confidence: low on specific server names).

RobotCombatWiki (robotcombatwiki.com) serves as the community's reference wiki, with a dedicated 'Plastic Antweights' page summarizing the class's rules and history, plus a general '3D Printing' page covering combat-specific printing advice that applies directly to this class.

Practical entry point for a new builder: starting from an existing open-source design (Force Knight, CRNZ's kit, or a Cults3D vertical spinner) and modifying it, rather than designing a first chassis from a blank CAD canvas, is both common and explicitly encouraged by how these designs are shared and licensed.
