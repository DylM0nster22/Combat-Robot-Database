Banned Materials and How Bans Are Written

Rules

plastic-antweightrulesbanned-materialstpunylonmagnets

The core ban across every ruleset reviewed is simple: any material used for chassis, armor, or weapon that is not on the approved plastics list. SPARC 4.4.1 spells this out with explicit examples — "No other types of plastics or materials allowed (ie. metal, carbon fiber, UHMW, etc)." SCAR's base ruleset repeats this trio (metal, carbon fiber, UHMW) verbatim.

SCAR's supplementary Plastic Ant FAQ goes further and names several materials that are technically 3D-printed plastics but are still banned because they sit outside the PLA/PETG/ABS/PET/ASA family or are seen as violating the class's spirit:

  • TPU (including custom TPU wheel hubs) — banned by name, despite being popular for flexible armor in the wider (metal-legal) antweight/beetleweight community.
  • Nylon — banned by name, despite being a tough, useful engineering filament in general 3D printing.
  • 'Flex' or blended filaments marketed for enhanced properties — banned as a category, to close the loophole of using an exotic blend that isn't literally named TPU or nylon but behaves the same way.
  • Composite filaments (carbon-fiber-filled or glass-filled plastics) — banned, consistent with the base ruleset's carbon-fiber ban.
  • Epoxy coatings, Bondo, or similar fillers — banned as post-processing 'armor' additions.
  • Rubber shock-mount armor ('wubs') and multi-layer tape armor — banned as improvised armor techniques borrowed from full-metal antweight practice that would circumvent the plastic-only spirit.

Magnets are separately and universally banned when used to enhance traction or downforce (SPARC 4.4.2, SCAR ruleset) — this is a general antweight-class magnet rule, not specific to plastic ants, but it applies here too.

Post-processing carve-outs: SCAR's FAQ explicitly allows annealing/heat-or-acid smoothing of a printed part, and thin decorative paint, distinguishing legitimate finishing techniques from material additives that change the part's mechanical properties or add foreign armor material.

The overall pattern: base rulesets ban by naming forbidden material categories (metal, carbon fiber, UHMW); FAQs and event-specific clarifications then ban by naming specific filaments and techniques that technically dodge the base wording but violate its intent. Builders bringing a design to a new event should always check for a supplementary FAQ, not just the headline ruleset.
