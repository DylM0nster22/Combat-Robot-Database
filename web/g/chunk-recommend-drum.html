Recommended weapon motor: 1 lb drum

Application-Specific Recommendations

antweightrecommendationdrum-spinner

Drum spinners (a full-width cylindrical drum with teeth along its length, spinning about a horizontal axis parallel to the ground and perpendicular to the direction of travel) are torque-hungry compared to a disc of similar weight, because a drum concentrates a large fraction of its mass at a moderate-to-large radius along its entire length, giving high MOI, and because drum weapons are typically run at deliberately low RPM/high-bite settings (drums rely on scooping/launching opponents more than pure kinetic penetration, so builders favor low tip speed and high torque over raw speed).

This pushes drum builds toward the lower-KV, larger-stator end of the antweight-to-heavy-ant bracket: a 2504 or 2508 at 1300-1700kv, or a 2806 at 1000-1400kv, direct-driving or lightly belt-reducing (1.3-2:1) the drum is a common, effective choice. The wider/taller stator of these sizes gives the stall torque needed to spin a drum's higher MOI up in a reasonable time and to recover quickly after every scoop/launch event, which functions almost like a repeated partial-stall on the motor.

Because drum motors are heavier (50-80g+) than the 22xx bracket used for discs, drum builds on a strict 1 lb budget usually compensate by keeping the drum itself relatively light-walled/short and by trimming weight elsewhere (thinner armor on non-weapon-facing panels, a lighter drivetrain), rather than trying to fit both a large heavy drum and a large heavy motor into 454g at the same time. A dead-shaft belt-drive layout is especially common on drum builds specifically because it lets the drum's own shaft/bearings (sized for the drum's load) be decoupled from the motor's shaft/bell, and because drum belt/pulley swaps in the pits are easier with a dead shaft than with a keyed rotating shaft running through the whole drum length.
