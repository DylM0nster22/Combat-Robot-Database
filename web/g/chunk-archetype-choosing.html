Choosing an archetype for your first antweight

Getting started

antweightplastic-antweightgetting-startedmeta

The archetype you pick determines what you spend your 454 g on, what you need to be able to build, and which opponents you will struggle against. For a first robot, the honest ranking is:

Build a control bot or wedge first if you have never driven a combat robot. Driving is the skill that decides more antweight matches than design does, and a control bot lets you practise it without a weapon that can destroy your own robot. You will also finish every match, which is how you learn what actually breaks.

Build a beater bar or small vertical spinner if you want a weapon and have a printer or basic tools. The beater bar is the cheapest real weapon: bar stock, a hub, a brushless motor. You get to learn spin-up management, bite and gyro handling on a robot that survives its own mistakes.

Build a drum if you are on your second or third robot and can machine or print accurately. Drums have the best bite of any spinner and hit upward, which is the damage vector a 1 lb robot cannot resist.

Avoid on a first build: shell spinners, melty brains, grabbers, crushers and flippers. Each has a specific hard problem — a large bearing race, custom firmware, a reliable grab, enormous force, a latch that survives impact — that is not a good place to learn.

A note on the meta: almost every top competitive antweight now looks like a vertical spinner with hinged wedgelets either side of the weapon. That combination exists because a bare spinner loses the ground game to a wedge and a bare wedge cannot generate damage. If you are optimising for winning rather than learning, that is the configuration to converge on.
