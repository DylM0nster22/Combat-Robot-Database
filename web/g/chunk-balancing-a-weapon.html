Balancing a weapon without a balancing machine

Weapon build

antweightplastic-antweightweapon-designmaintenancefabrication

An unbalanced weapon at antweight speeds is destructive out of proportion to how small the imbalance looks. At 20,000 rpm a fraction of a gram off-centre generates enough vibration to back fasteners out across the whole robot, brinell the weapon bearings within a single match, and make the robot genuinely hard to drive.

Static balancing, which is what you can do at home:

  1. Assemble the weapon on its shaft exactly as it will run, including the hub and any fasteners.
  2. Rest the shaft ends on two level, parallel hard edges — knife edges, two lengths of angle, or the parallel jaws of a vice set slightly apart.
  3. Let it settle. The heavy side rolls to the bottom.
  4. Rotate 90 degrees and release again. Repeat. If it consistently stops in the same orientation, it is out of balance.
  5. Remove material from the heavy side rather than adding it to the light side — adding mass increases inertia and makes any residual imbalance worse. Drill a shallow hole, or file, near the rim on the heavy side.
  6. Repeat until the weapon stops in a different position each time.

What this misses. Static balancing cannot detect dynamic imbalance, where the weapon is balanced about its axis but its mass is distributed unevenly along the shaft, producing a wobble rather than a vibration. For the short, disc-like weapons typical at antweight this is usually a minor effect; for a long drum it can matter.

Design so it stays balanced. Symmetric weapons balance naturally. A single-tooth disc needs a deliberate counterweight on the opposite side. Teeth that bolt on should be paired, and if you replace one you should replace or re-match its opposite.

Re-balance after every significant impact. A bent weapon is an unbalanced weapon. This is one of the between-match checks that is genuinely worth the time — run the weapon up briefly and listen and feel for new vibration, and if anything changed, check it on the edges before the next match.
