Using the arena

Driving

antweightplastic-antweightdrivingstrategy

The arena is a weapon that costs no weight. Most antweight drivers under-use it.

Walls. A robot held against a wall cannot manoeuvre, cannot spin up properly, and often cannot escape. Pinning against a wall is the highest-value control action available to a weaponless robot, and it stops a spinner's weapon from being useful. Learn where the walls are without looking at them.

Corners. A corner is better than a wall because the opponent loses two escape directions. Driving an opponent into a corner and holding them is close to a win on control.

Pushouts, where the ruleset allows them. Not every arena has them. Where one exists, a control robot can win in seconds against anything. Check before the event whether your arena has a pushout rule and how low the wall is.

Hazards and the pit. Where present, these convert a control advantage into an immediate win. A grabber or lifter that can carry an opponent should be aiming at the pit from the first second.

The floor. Arena floors are not perfectly flat. Seams, dents and paint changes all affect a low wedge or an undercutter. Walk the arena before your match if you are allowed, and notice where the floor is bad — then fight your opponent over that spot.

Your own position. Avoid fighting with your back to a wall against a pushing robot; you have nowhere to retreat and no room to build speed. Equally, do not sit in the centre against a spinner — you have no wall to use and they have every approach angle.

A practical drill: in practice, drive the perimeter of your arena in both directions, inverted and upright, until you can do it without looking away from the robot. Wall awareness is a skill and it transfers directly into match control.
