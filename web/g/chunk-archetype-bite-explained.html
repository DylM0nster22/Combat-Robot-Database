Why bite decides whether your spinner actually works

Spinners

antweightplastic-antweightspinnerphysicsdamage

Two robots can store identical energy and produce completely different results, because energy only matters if the weapon engages. That is bite.

When two robots close at speed v and a weapon has t teeth turning at N rpm, teeth arrive at a rate of N/60 * t per second. The deepest a tooth can possibly reach is the distance the robots close between arrivals:

bite_depth = v / (N/60 * t)

Put real numbers in. A 20,000 rpm weapon with two teeth, closing at 1.5 m/s, presents 667 teeth per second and can bite 2.25 mm. The same weapon with six teeth bites 0.75 mm — likely less than the opponent's armour thickness, so it skates off and converts its energy into noise and sparks.

This is counter-intuitive and it explains a lot of antweight design:

  • Fewer teeth hit harder. Most competitive discs run one or two effective teeth.
  • Slower can hit harder. Dropping weapon rpm increases bite. Past a point, spinning faster makes you less effective.
  • Closing speed is part of your weapon. Driving into the opponent increases bite. Sitting still and waiting reduces it to zero.
  • Drums bite best. A drum's large diameter and low mounting mean the drum body itself wedges under the opponent, forcing engagement rather than hoping for it.

Bite is also why the opponent's wedge is such an effective defence: a wedge presents a ramp that the tooth glances off, and raises the contact point away from the bite-favourable geometry.
