Tournament strategy across a bracket

Strategy

antweightplastic-antweightstrategyevents

A single match and a whole bracket call for different decisions.

Your robot has to survive the day, not the match. A robot destroyed winning round one is worth less than a robot that tapped out and fought three more times. Tapping out is a legitimate strategic tool, and most rulesets provide for it. If you are clearly losing and the robot is being destroyed, conceding preserves it.

Know the format. Double elimination means an early loss is survivable and you should take risks less readily in the losers' bracket, where one more loss ends your day. Round-robin means every match counts equally and consistency beats brilliance. A single-elimination bracket means a conservative, reliable robot is worth more than a fragile high-damage one.

Manage your spares across the day. If you have one spare weapon and five matches, do not use it in round one because the weapon is slightly bent. Triage: fix what stops the robot working, live with what is merely ugly.

Scout your side of the bracket. You can usually see two rounds ahead. If your likely next opponent is a horizontal spinner and you have a taller wedge in the pit, fit it before that match rather than during it.

Battery discipline. Rotate packs so every match starts on a full one and nothing gets deep-discharged. Label them. This sounds trivial and it decides matches.

Pace yourself. Events run long, pits are hot, and mistakes made at hour eight are the ones that lose brackets. Eat, drink water, and do your between-match checklist even when you are tired — especially then.

The conservative option is usually right. At antweight, the robot that reliably finishes matches accumulates wins against flashier opponents that break. Across a full bracket, reliability compounds.
