Recommended weapon motor: 1 lb beater bar

Application-Specific Recommendations

antweightplastic-antweightrecommendationbeater-bareggbeater

A beater bar (sometimes called an eggbeater) is a vertical-axis weapon with two or more flat/angled paddles rather than a solid disc, usually run at a lower RPM and higher torque than a disc for the same rough tip speed target, because the individual paddles need to survive a much more concentrated, repeated impact than a continuous disc rim. Beater bars are popular on both plastic antweights and 1 lb metal antweights because a commercial beater-bar kit (blade plus a dead-shaft hex-bolt shaft arrangement) is one of the simplest weapons to bolt onto almost any drivetrain-only base kit.

Motor sizing follows the same math as a disc (tip-speed and required-KV formulas apply identically), but builders commonly target the lower end of the usual 200-250 mph range, and lean toward a slightly lower KV / more torque-biased motor than they would choose for an equivalent-diameter disc, because a beater bar's paddles present more of an impulsive, tooth-like load per revolution (closer to a low-tooth-count drum than a smooth disc) and benefit from the extra bite that comes with somewhat lower RPM and higher available torque. For a plastic antweight beater bar, a 1408 in the 2200-3000kv range at 3-4S is a common, well-proven choice; for a full 1 lb metal antweight beater bar, builders typically step up to an 1806 or 2205 in the 1700-2300kv range at 4S for extra stall torque and shaft strength, since beater-bar impacts tend to load the shaft with more off-axis/bending force than a flat, evenly-loaded disc does.

Because many commercial beater-bar weapon kits use a dead-shaft design (a fixed hex-bolt shaft with the blade/hub spinning around it on bearings, driven by a belt from the motor), the motor itself is usually isolated from direct impact shock in these builds, which is one reason beater bars have a reputation as a relatively motor-friendly weapon choice for newer antweight builders compared to a harsh direct-drive disc.
