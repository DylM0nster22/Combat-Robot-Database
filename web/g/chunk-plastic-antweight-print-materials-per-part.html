Print Material Choice Per Part

Printing

plastic-antweightprintingmaterial-selection

Not every part of a plastic ant should be printed in the same filament; matching material properties to each part's job is a core competitive skill in this class.

PLA / PLA+ — chassis shells, low-stress structural parts, prototypes. PLA is the easiest of the approved materials to print reliably (low warp, no enclosure needed, prints cool at 190-215C) and is fine for parts that see moderate load but aren't the primary impact-absorbing structure — electronics trays, standoffs, brackets, and early prototype iterations where print speed and reliability matter more than ultimate toughness. PLA+ specifically markets improved impact resistance over plain PLA while keeping PLA's easy-print behavior, making it a reasonable default for a first build's chassis.

PETG — weapon discs/drums, load-bearing chassis rails, anything expected to take a direct hit. PETG's more ductile failure mode (it bends and cracks slowly rather than shattering) makes it the community's most-recommended choice for any part that will actually absorb combat impact, at the cost of slightly trickier print tuning (more prone to stringing, needs dialed-in retraction settings) and marginally higher density (1.27 g/cm3 vs PLA's 1.24 g/cm3).

ABS — weight-critical parts, acetone-smoothed weapons. ABS is the lowest-density of the commonly used approved plastics (1.04 g/cm3) and can be acetone-vapor-smoothed, which welds surface layer lines together for extra strength at the outer surface — a real advantage for a weapon disc's outer rim. The tradeoff is that ABS needs an enclosed, heated-chamber printer to avoid warping, making it a more advanced-builder's material choice than PLA or PETG.

TPU — flexible armor/tires, WHERE LOCALLY LEGAL ONLY. TPU is not on the SPARC baseline list and is explicitly banned by SCAR's FAQ; never design it into a competition part without confirming the specific event's rules first (see the dedicated TPU/org-variance discussion elsewhere in this file).

Nylon — generally avoid for competition use. Nylon's toughness makes it tempting for gears/wear parts, but it sits outside the base approved list and is explicitly banned by SCAR by name; its moisture sensitivity also makes it one of the harder approved-adjacent materials to print reliably even where it might be allowed.

Practical per-part guide: chassis body → PLA+ or PETG; weapon disc/drum → PETG (or well-annealed ABS for advanced builders); wheel hub → PLA+ or PETG (any of the rigid approved plastics work); armor shell over electronics → PETG for its ductility; brackets/standoffs → PLA is fine, they rarely take direct impact.
