Your first event: what to bring and what happens

Events

antweightplastic-antweighteventsgetting-started

Before you go. Weigh the robot on your own scale and build to about 445 g, not 454 g. Set and test your failsafe. Fit a weapon lock. Charge every battery and bring them at storage voltage if you are travelling. Print or bookmark the event's ruleset.

What to pack. The list that actually matters:

  • Spare weapon, already balanced
  • Spare wheels and hubs
  • Spare battery, at least two, ideally four
  • The exact drivers and hex keys your robot uses, plus spares of the sizes that strip
  • Soldering iron, solder, heat-shrink, wire
  • Threadlocker (blue), superglue, zip ties, electrical tape
  • Spare fasteners in every size the robot uses
  • Multimeter
  • Your transmitter, its charger, and a spare receiver if you have one
  • LiPo-safe bag
  • Spare printed parts if it is a plastic ant — print a whole spare chassis

What happens on the day. You arrive, check in, and go to safety inspection. The inspector will check weight, the removable link or power switch, the weapon lock, and your failsafe — they will usually ask you to power up, then turn the transmitter off, and watch that everything stops. Then you wait, fight your matches with variable amounts of warning, and repair between them.

What actually goes wrong for first-timers: overweight at check-in; no failsafe set; no weapon lock; a battery that was not charged; a transmitter that was not bound to the right model memory; and forgetting a tool that only fits one fastener on the robot.

What to expect of yourself. You will probably lose. Almost everyone does at their first event, and the robots that win have usually been to several. The value is in watching what breaks, seeing what the good robots do differently, and talking to other builders — who are, almost universally, generous with help and spare parts.
