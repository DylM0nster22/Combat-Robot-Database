Choosing 2S vs 3S vs 4S for an Antweight

Cell Count

antweightplastic-antweightcell-countvoltageweapon-motorkv

Cell count sets the pack's nominal voltage: 2S = 7.4V, 3S = 11.1V, 4S = 14.8V (add roughly 0.1V/cell for LiHV packs at the same series count). For a fixed weapon motor, more cells means proportionally more no-load RPM (RPM ~= KV * Voltage), so builders pick cell count and motor KV together, not independently.

2S (7.4V) is the traditional antweight baseline, especially for control bots, wedges, and lower-KV weapon setups. It asks the least of the pack (lowest voltage means lower current for a given power level is not automatically true — power = VI, so a 2S system needs more* current than a 3S system for the same weapon power) and is the lightest option cell-for-cell, but a 2S pack has to work harder (more amps) to deliver the same wattage as a higher cell count, which increases both voltage sag and wiring/connector demands.

3S (11.1V) is the most common sweet spot for antweight spinners today. Popular weapon motors in the 2000-2600KV range paired with 3S land in a practical tip-speed/torque range for a 1 lb disc or drum, and 3S packs in the 300-550mAh range keep total weight reasonable. As one antweight motor example puts it, a 2400KV motor on 3S yields roughly 25,000+ RPM no-load — already fast enough that most builders don't need to go to 4S with that motor.

4S (14.8V) shows up on plastic antweights and metal antweights chasing higher tip speed with lower-KV motors (roughly 1000-1800KV) to avoid overspeeding a given weapon geometry, or on drivetrains wanting more headroom before voltage sag becomes limiting. The tradeoff is weight and risk: a 4S pack of a given capacity weighs more than an equivalent-Wh 3S or 2S pack (an extra series cell is roughly a flat 20-30% weight adder over 3S for the same mAh rating), and pairing a high-KV motor with 4S without checking the math is a documented way to overspeed and burn out a weapon motor — one antweight builder specifically warned that a 2600KV motor on 4S would not survive, while the 1250KV version of the same motor was fine.

Rule of thumb weight penalty per cell: going from 2S to 3S at the same mAh rating typically adds 30-50% pack weight; 3S to 4S adds a further 20-30%. Because weight is the antweight's tightest constraint, most builders choose the lowest cell count that lets their chosen weapon motor reach the RPM/tip-speed they want, rather than defaulting to 4S for headroom.
