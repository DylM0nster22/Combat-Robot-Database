Outrunner vs inrunner motors for weapons

Weapon Motor Fundamentals

antweightplastic-antweightoutrunnerinrunnermotor-topology

Brushless motors come in two basic mechanical layouts, and combat weapon drives overwhelmingly use one of them.

Outrunner: the permanent magnets are glued around the inside of an outer bell/can, which spins around a stationary inner stator wound with copper. This is the layout used by essentially every FPV drone motor (11xx through 28xx alike) and therefore by nearly every combat weapon motor discussed in this file. Outrunners have two big advantages for weapon use: first, the magnets sit at a large radius from the shaft, giving a long moment arm that produces more torque per amp than an inrunner of similar size -- exactly what a direct-drive weapon wants, since there's no gearbox to multiply torque. Second, the spinning outer bell is itself a natural weapon-mounting surface and adds usable rotating mass (flywheel effect) to the weapon system for free, which inrunners can't offer since their rotor is buried inside a fixed can.

Inrunner: the permanent-magnet rotor is a small cylinder spinning inside a fixed outer stator can, the classic layout for RC car and boat brushless motors. Inrunners run at much higher KV for a given physical size and need external gear reduction to produce useful torque at low speed, plus a way to attach the weapon to a shaft rather than to the motor's own housing. Because combat weapons want naturally low-KV, torque-biased motors close to direct-drive speeds, inrunners are a poor match for the antweight bread-and-butter application, and are essentially absent from weapon-motor discussions in the hobby (they do occasionally appear in drivetrain use, which is a separate topic).

The practical upshot: unless a build has an unusual reason to introduce a gearbox and separate weapon shaft anyway (in which case an inrunner could theoretically work), outrunner FPV-style motors are the default and near-universal choice for antweight and plastic-antweight weapon drives, and every motor cataloged in this file's size ladder is an outrunner.
