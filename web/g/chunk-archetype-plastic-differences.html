How the archetype picture changes in plastic ant

Plastic ant

plastic-antweightplastic-antmetamaterials

The plastic antweight class changes the relative value of the archetypes, because the weapon can no longer be made of metal.

Damage becomes blunt force. A printed tooth cannot cut. What a plastic weapon does is deliver impulse — it knocks the opponent around, breaks printed parts by shock, and occasionally cracks a chassis. The archetypes that rely on cutting or piercing (saws, crushers) become almost pointless. The ones that rely on delivering impulse upward (vertical spinners, drums, beater bars) still work.

Compliance beats hardness. In metal ant you armour with titanium or UHMW. In plastic ant, TPU armour is the strong choice, because a flexible panel spreads the impact over time and returns energy rather than fracturing. Rigid PLA armour cracks; an 85A-95A TPU panel bounces.

Spinners hurt themselves more. A printed weapon taking a hard impact fails at the layer lines. Weapons become consumables, which is fine — that is the point of the class — but it shifts the design toward cheap, fast-to-reprint weapons rather than precious ones.

Control archetypes gain value. Because damage is harder to achieve, more matches go to decisions and to ring-outs. A well-driven plastic wedge or control bot is proportionally stronger than its metal-ant equivalent.

Horizontal spinners get worse. Their recoil problem is unchanged, but their ability to convert that recoil into opponent damage is reduced. Vertical spinners and drums dominate the class more completely than they do in metal ant.

One rules caveat worth knowing: organisations disagree about post-processing. SPARC's plastic class explicitly bans annealing, acetone smoothing and boiling as durability-enhancing treatments, while some other events permit them. A weapon that is legal at one event can be illegal at another, so check before you print.
