Battery and ESC sizing: C-rating, voltage sag, and current headroom

Electrical Fundamentals

antweightplastic-antweightelectricalbatteryescfundamentals

Sizing the electrical system starts from the battery. formula-battery-c-rating-to-amps converts a pack's label rating into a real continuous-current number: a common 300 mAh (0.3 Ah) 2S pack rated 60C gives I_continuous = 600.3 = 18 A. formula-battery-wh-capacity gives its total stored energy on an equal footing across cell counts: Wh = VAh = 7.4*0.3 = 2.22 Wh for that same 2S pack.

Voltage sag is the detail most commonly skipped. formula-voltage-sag: V_loaded = V_nominal - I_draw*R_internal. With a typical small high-C pack's R_internal = 0.020 ohm, drawing a modest 10 A sags the 7.4 V pack to 7.20 V, and drawing the full rated 18 A sags it to 7.04 V -- a ~5% voltage drop exactly when the weapon or drivetrain needs full torque most (during a stall or hard impact). Since motor torque and power both scale with applied voltage, ignoring sag means overestimating real available torque right when it matters.

ESC sizing should headroom well above stall current, not cruise current (formula-esc-current-headroom): a weapon motor with a 30 A stall current should sit behind an ESC rated at least 1.5-2x that (52.5-60 A), since weapon motors stall briefly on every real hit -- an ESC sized only to the motor's typical cruise draw will brown out or cook itself the first time the weapon connects solidly.

Efficiency is not a fixed number (formula-motor-efficiency-estimate): eta = P_out/P_in = (Tomega)/(VI) varies across the motor's speed range. In the worked weapon-motor example, efficiency at the 20,000 rpm operating point came out to 65.5% -- useful for estimating how much of the battery's Wh capacity actually turns into weapon KE and run time, rather than assuming a fixed number pulled from a datasheet's single peak-efficiency point.

Putting it together for run time: with an average combined current draw of 8 A (mixing idle weapon-maintain current with occasional drive current) and the 0.3 Ah pack above, runtime = Capacity_Ah/I_avg = 0.3/8 = 0.0375 h = 2.25 minutes -- worth checking explicitly against the typical 2-3 minute fight length, since it's easy to assume a battery's mAh number is "plenty" without doing this division.
