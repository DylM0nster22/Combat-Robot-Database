Recommended weapon motor: 1 lb horizontal bar

Application-Specific Recommendations

antweightrecommendationhorizontal-spinnerbar

Horizontal bar spinners (a bar rotating about a roughly horizontal axis across the width of the robot) face a fundamentally different load than a thin vertical disc: much higher aerodynamic drag from the bar's flat faces pushing air, plus a bar shape that typically carries more of its mass further from the axis (higher MOI per unit weight) than a compact disc. Two practical consequences drive motor choice: continuous current tends to run higher for a given RPM than a disc of similar weight (because of drag), and torque demand during spin-up is higher (because of MOI), so builders deliberately favor lower KV than a vertical-disc build of the same size class to keep both current and spin-up time under control -- purpose-built horizontal-spinner motors like the JCR-4935 are specifically wound to a comparatively low 1000kv for exactly this reason, well below what a same-size vertical-disc motor would typically run.

For a direct-drive 1 lb horizontal bar, a 2206 or 2306 in a 1300-1700kv winding on 4S is a common, solid choice -- enough stator volume for real torque and thermal margin against sustained drag load, at a KV low enough to avoid overspeeding into a drag wall where added RPM stops translating into added tip speed efficiently. If the desired bar length/mass pushes tip speed targets down further (very common for full-width bars, which are usually heavier and slower-spinning by design than a compact disc), a belt-drive setup with something in the 2306-2504 range paired with a 1.5-2:1 reduction is a common step up, since it lets the motor run at a more efficient point on its torque-speed curve while still hitting a modest bar tip-speed target.

Because horizontal bars see sustained drag-driven current even at steady RPM (unlike a disc, which mostly only draws heavy current during spin-up and stalls), thermal margin matters more here than for a disc build of similar weapon mass -- favor the larger, cooler-running end of whatever KV/size bracket you land on, and budget real ESC current headroom (see formula-power-draw) rather than sizing the ESC to only the motor's brief peak.
