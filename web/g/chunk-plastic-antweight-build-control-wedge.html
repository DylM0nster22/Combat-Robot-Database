Design rationale: a control bot deliberately spends almost nothing on 'weapon' (just an optional small active-wedge servo) and instead maximizes armor thickness and low-CG ballast to win pushing matches and resist being flipped. The printed ballast plug is a simple, cheap, 100%-legal way to use the full weight budget when there's no weapon consuming it — solid PETG at high infill is denser and cheaper than any COTS metal ballast option would be to source, and it's trivially plastic-legal since it's just more of the same approved material.

Weight budget as percentages: armor/structure (chassis+wedge+shell = 250g) ≈ 55%, drive (motors+wheels+combo board = 48g) ≈ 11%, weapon/active-wedge servo (15g) ≈ 3%, electronics (battery+wiring = 34g) ≈ 8%, fasteners ≈ 3%, ballast (90g) ≈ 20%.

Why this archetype is recommended as a first build: every failure mode here (a cracked wedge, a worn wheel, a stripped screw hole) is a five-minute reprint or hardware-store fix, unlike a broken weapon motor mount or a snapped weapon shaft, which are common failure points on a first spinner build and are considerably more frustrating and costly to diagnose and fix mid-tournament.
