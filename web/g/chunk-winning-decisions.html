Winning a decision you did not dominate

Strategy

antweightplastic-antweightstrategyrules

Most antweight matches that do not end in a knockout end in a judges' decision, and decisions are winnable by design as well as by performance.

Understand the split. Most rulesets score damage, aggression and control, with damage weighted heaviest. Check your event's published weighting — it varies, and a control-heavy ruleset changes your whole strategy.

Damage is what the judges saw. A hit that visibly removes a panel, bends a weapon or stops a wheel scores. A hit that dents armour invisibly does not. If you are a spinner, aim for things that come off: wedgelets, armour panels, wheels. Visible damage is scored damage.

Aggression means initiating, not flailing. Judges reward the robot that goes forward and makes the match happen. Spinning in place waiting to be approached scores badly even if it is tactically sound. If you must play defensively, at least drive toward the opponent between engagements.

Control means the match went where you wanted. Pins, carries, pushing the opponent into hazards, and controlling the centre of the arena all count. A control bot that carries the opponent around for three minutes has a real case even with no damage.

Practical tactics for a close decision:

  • Finish strong. Judges remember the last thirty seconds disproportionately. If you are unsure, be aggressive at the end.
  • Do not hide. Running away for the last minute loses more decisions than it saves robots — unless you are protecting a robot that needs to survive to the next round, which is a legitimate tournament trade.
  • Make your damage visible. Hitting the same spot repeatedly produces one obvious failure rather than five invisible dents.
  • Do not argue with judges. It has never worked and it affects how you are treated next time.

The meta-point: if you build a robot with no damage output, you have chosen to win every close match on aggression and control. That is possible, but it means you must drive forward constantly.
