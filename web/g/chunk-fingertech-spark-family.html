FingerTech Silver Spark and Gold Spark: The Ratio Ladder Explained

Drive Motors

antweightplastic-antweightdrive-motorfingertechgear-ratiosilver-sparkgold-spark

Both FingerTech Spark motors share the exact same base motor: a Mabuchi FK-050 brushed can rated for roughly 11,530 RPM no-load and 1.3A stall current at the 7.4V (2S LiPo) voltage most antweights run on, though FingerTech states it will run anywhere from 3V to 18.5V. What differs between Silver and Gold Spark — and between the ratios within each line — is entirely in the gearbox.

Silver Spark uses an all-metal spur gearhead across nine stock ratios: 11.1, 22.2, 33.3, 50, 83.3 (discontinued), 100, 200, 300, and 600:1. Weight climbs gently with ratio (28g at 11:1 up to 32g at 600:1) because the gearbox needs progressively more/larger gear stages to reach the higher reductions, but the motor and price stay constant across the whole family. The output shaft is a 3mm-diameter flatted shaft, standard length 38mm, with a shorter 22mm option on the 22:1/33:1/50:1 ratios for tighter builds — long enough that a wheel can be mounted directly onto it (a live-axle setup) without an intermediate shaft or bearing block, though FingerTech does offer an optional bearing block for extra support on the far side of the wheel.

Gold Spark uses the identical base motor but a gearbox with a plastic final gear stage, across a slightly different eight-ratio ladder: 20, 35, 50, 86, 115, 150, 250, and 360:1, all at a constant 28g and roughly 25% lower price than Silver Spark. FingerTech explicitly documents a mechanical ceiling on Gold Spark: output torque above 6.84 kg-cm (95 oz-in) will damage the plastic output gear stage, which caps how much of the higher-ratio end of the ladder (250:1, 360:1) can actually be exploited before self-destructing under stall.

Because no-load RPM and stall current are properties of the shared base motor, output RPM at any given ratio is simply 11,530 divided by that ratio, and output stall torque is (very roughly) the bare-motor stall torque (~0.046 kg-cm) multiplied by the ratio and by gearbox efficiency (~0.75 for Silver Spark's metal spur stages, somewhat lower for Gold Spark's plastic stage). This lets a builder pick a ratio directly from a target speed or torque without needing FingerTech's full published table for every combination. In practice, 22.2:1 through 50:1 covers most competitive antweight main-drive builds — fast enough to dodge and reposition, with enough torque margin over the traction limit to push confidently — while 100:1 and above are reserved for weapon lifters, self-righters, or bots that specifically prioritize crawling torque over speed.
