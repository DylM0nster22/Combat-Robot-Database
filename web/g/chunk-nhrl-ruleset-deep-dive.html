NHRL: Cash-Prize Tournament Rules for 3/12/30 lb Classes

Major Rulesets

The National Havoc Robot League (NHRL) runs the highest-frequency, highest-production hobby combat robotics series in the US, based in Franklin, Massachusetts, and is notable for being one of the few organizations to pay real cash prizes to winners and runners-up rather than running purely for trophies/bragging rights. NHRL deliberately keeps its class list narrow — exactly three weight classes: 3 lb (branded internally alongside 'beetleweight'), 12 lb ('hobbyweight'), and 30 lb ('featherweight') — which lets it build a deep, well-refined ruleset and arena for each class rather than spreading effort across a dozen weight tiers.

Each class carries two distinct weight bonuses. 'Non-Traditional Locomotion' (any drive that isn't simple wheels, but doesn't rise to the strict walker definition) is worth a 50% weight bonus: +1.5 lb in the 3 lb class, +6 lb in the 12 lb class, +15 lb in the 30 lb class. 'True Walker' status is worth a full 100% bonus, doubling the class weight outright: +3 lb, +12 lb, and +30 lb respectively. This two-tier bonus structure mirrors what most other US rulesets do, just documented with unusually explicit numbers.

Safety and inspection requirements are strict and specifically tested at check-in: every robot must carry a weapon lock that physically prevents any spinning weapon from moving outside the arena cage, must have a way to fully power down without disassembly (a switch or removable link operable without tools), and must pass an active radio fail-safe test where an inspector or the driver deliberately kills the transmitter to confirm the robot goes fully inert.

Matches run 3 minutes. NHRL's tournament format is genuinely two-stage rather than a single bracket: a Qualifying stage first, where a robot needs two wins across up to three preliminary rounds (Opening/Round 1, then either The Cusp or Redemption/Round 2, and potentially a decisive Bubble/Round 3) to advance into the actual single-elimination Bracket stage. The top 50% of entrants, ranked by NHRL's ongoing ranking system, are seeded directly rather than starting from the bottom of the qualifying ladder — rewarding consistent past performance across the league's many events per year. A match itself ends by tap-out, knockout (extended controlled immobility), or, if the full 3 minutes elapses with both robots still mobile, a judges' decision.
