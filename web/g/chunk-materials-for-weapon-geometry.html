Materials in weapon geometry: matching the material to the part's job

Attachment and Failure

The geometry decisions covered throughout this topic -- rim-loading, tooth inserts vs monolithic construction, shaft sizing, hub attachment -- all assume specific material properties, and picking the right material for each specific part (rather than one material for the whole weapon) is often as important as the geometry itself.

Striking edges: hardness over toughness, within limits. The actual tooth or edge that contacts the opponent benefits most from hardness and wear resistance. material-s7-tool-steel is a common antweight choice for bolt-in tooth inserts specifically because it can be hardened into the low-to-mid 50s HRC range while retaining enough toughness to survive repeated impact without becoming brittle -- pushing hardness much higher trades away that impact toughness and risks a tooth that chips or shatters on a hard hit rather than simply dulling.

Weapon bodies and shafts: balanced strength and machinability. material-4140-steel is a common choice for weapon bodies, bars, and shafts where the part needs good strength and toughness without necessarily being through-hardened to striking-edge hardness -- it machines well in its annealed/normalized state and can be selectively heat-treated further (e.g., just the shaft) if needed.

Hubs and backing structure: strength-to-weight over hardness. material-7075-aluminum shows up frequently in hubs, backing plates, and structural weapon components sitting behind a hardened insert, where the part needs to be strong and stiff but isn't itself the striking surface -- its lower toughness compared to steel makes it a poor choice as a bare direct-impact edge, but a good one for everything supporting that edge.

Plastic-antweight bodies. material-uhmw-polyethylene is the default choice for plastic-antweight disc and drum bodies specifically because most plastic-antweight rulesets restrict the bulk weapon/structure material to non-metal while still permitting small hardened inserts, fasteners, motors, and bearings in metal -- always confirm the exact current material rule for a given event's plastic-antweight class before finalizing a design, since specifics vary by organization.

Weight-critical components. material-titanium-6al4v earns its place in weight-critical bars, hubs, or tip inserts where its roughly steel-level strength at about 60% of steel's density lets a builder redirect saved grams into more weapon mass at the rim (per the mass-toward-rim principle) or more drivetrain/battery budget elsewhere -- at the cost of being noticeably harder and slower to machine well without the right tooling, which makes it more of an advanced-builder material than a default first choice.

The general principle. Rather than asking 'what's the best weapon material,' the more useful antweight-scale question is which specific part -- edge, body, shaft, hub -- benefits most from hardness, from toughness, from strength-to-weight, or from being non-metal by rule, and matching each part's material to that specific job rather than defaulting to one material for the whole assembly.
