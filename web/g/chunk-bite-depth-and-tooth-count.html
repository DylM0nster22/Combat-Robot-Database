Bite depth and tooth count: the hit-frequency trade-off

Weapon Geometry Fundamentals

antweightplastic-antweightbiteweapon-geometrytooth-countfundamentals

"Bite" refers to how deep a spinning weapon's tooth digs into an opponent's material before the next tooth strike arrives -- and it depends on three things: how fast the two robots are closing together, how fast the weapon spins, and how many teeth it has.

The core relation (formula-bite-depth): hit frequency f_hit = (rpm/60)*tooth_count, and bite_depth = v_closing/f_hit. At 20,000 rpm with a single tooth and a 1.5 m/s closing speed, f_hit = 333.3 hits/s and bite_depth = 1.5/333.3 = 4.5 mm engaged per hit.

Adding a second tooth at the same RPM doubles the hit frequency and HALVES the bite depth (formula-hit-frequency): the same weapon with 2 teeth at the same 20,000 rpm gives f_hit = 666.7 hits/s and bite_depth = 1.5/666.7 = 2.25 mm -- half the per-hit penetration, for twice the hit rate.

This is the physical reason single-tooth designs dominate hard-hitting vertical spinners: for a given weapon RPM and closing speed, fewer teeth means a longer gap between successive strikes, which means more material gets engaged (and more damage delivered) per individual hit -- at the cost of a lower overall hit rate and, if the tooth is mounted further out to add MOI, a longer spin-up time (see formula-parallel-axis-theorem). Multi-tooth designs trade this away for a higher, steadier hit rate and (often) a smoother-spinning, easier-to-balance weapon, which suits control-oriented or lower-power builds where sheer per-hit penetration matters less than reliable, frequent contact.

Practical use: given an opponent's known or estimated armor thickness, this formula can be run in reverse to check whether a given tooth-count/RPM combination will actually penetrate on a single pass, or whether the design needs either a slower/single-tooth weapon for deeper bite, or simply relies on cumulative multi-hit damage instead.
