Structural math for impact survival: force, stress, bending, and why compliance helps

Structural Fundamentals

antweightplastic-antweightstructureimpactarmorfundamentals

Impact survival math starts from the impulse-momentum relation, formula-impact-force-momentum-time: F_avg = delta_p/delta_t = m_eff*delta_v/delta_t. A weapon's effective mass (0.03174 kg, from formula-effective-mass-at-tooth) losing 10 m/s of tip speed over a brief 1.5 ms contact produces an average force around 212 N -- a genuinely large force from a genuinely small, light part, entirely because the contact time is so short.

That force then has to be checked against real materials and geometry. formula-stress-force-area (sigma = F/A) shows the same 212 N spread over a small 25 mm^2 contact patch produces about 8.5 MPa of stress -- fine against aluminum's ~276 MPa yield strength, but potentially enough to locally dent a softer plastic panel at that same tiny contact area. For an actual armor panel bolted at two edges, formula-beam-bending-armor-panel models it as a simply-supported beam under a midspan point load: a 3mm-thick, 50mm-wide aluminum panel spanning 40mm between mounts under that same 212 N hit sees about 28.2 MPa peak bending stress -- roughly a 9.8x safety margin against yield, comfortably surviving the hit.

Why compliance (a little "give") helps (formula-compliance-reduces-peak-force): for the SAME momentum change, doubling the contact/stopping time exactly halves the peak force (F = delta_p/delta_t). A rigid mount stopping the same impact in 1.5 ms sees 212 N; a compliant standoff or slightly flexible frame stretching that to 3.0 ms sees only about 106 N -- half the peak force, for physically the same hit. This is why armor and weapon mounts that allow a small amount of flex often outlast perfectly rigid ones: they're not absorbing less energy, they're absorbing the SAME energy over more time, which is what peak force (and therefore cracking/yielding) actually cares about.

Fasteners get checked the same way (formula-bolt-shear-estimate): two M3 bolts sharing that 212 N reaction force see about 15.0 MPa of shear stress each, versus a typical alloy-steel bolt's 350-500 MPa shear strength -- over 20x margin on a single hit, though real mounts should still account for fatigue from repeated hits across a fight, not just a single worst-case load.
