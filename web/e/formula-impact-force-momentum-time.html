Impact force from momentum change and contact time

The average force during an impact equals the momentum change divided by how long the impact takes -- the key reason very brief, stiff impacts produce enormous peak forces compared to the same momentum change spread over a longer contact time (see formula-compliance-reduces-peak-force).

Formulaantweightplastic-antweightstructureimpact-forcemomentum

Expression

F_avg = delta_p / delta_t = m_eff * delta_v / delta_t

Variables

F_avgaverage force during the impact, N
delta_pmomentum change during the impact, kg*m/s
m_effeffective mass involved (e.g. weapon effective mass at the tip), kg
delta_vvelocity change during the impact, m/s
delta_tcontact/impact duration, s

Worked example

Weapon effective mass m_eff = 0.03174 kg slows from v = 67.0 m/s to about 57.0 m/s tip speed during a hit (delta_v = 10.0 m/s, consistent with the 20,000->17,000 rpm drop used in formula-energy-per-hit-vs-stored), over a contact time delta_t = 0.0015 s (1.5 ms, typical for a hard metal-on-metal impact). delta_p = 0.03174 10.0 = 0.3174 kgm/s. F_avg = 0.3174 / 0.0015 = 211.6 N.
