ABS / ASA Filament

Acrylonitrile butadiene styrene and its UV-stable cousin ASA — tougher and more heat-resistant than PLA, but needs an enclosure/heated bed to avoid warping, and its layer bonds are a common weak point in impact.

Materialantweightplastic-antweight3d-printedwarp-proneenclosure-needed

Strengths

  • Higher heat deflection temperature than PLA/PETG — holds up near hot motors/ESCs
  • Tougher overall than PLA
  • ASA adds strong UV/weather resistance ABS lacks
  • Acetone vapor smoothing can seal and strengthen ABS layer lines

Weaknesses

  • Prone to warping/cracking without a heated bed and enclosure
  • Layer adhesion is more failure-prone than PLA if printed without proper temperature control — a common combat-robot weak point is a shell splitting along a layer line under impact
  • Fumes during printing (needs ventilation)

Notes

ABS/ASA is chosen over PLA mainly for heat tolerance (parts mounted near a hot weapon motor or ESC) or UV/weather resistance (ASA), not generally for extra impact toughness — its main practical risk in combat use is inter-layer delamination if the enclosure/temperature setup wasn't dialed in, since ABS is more sensitive to print-cooling-induced weak layer bonds than PLA.
