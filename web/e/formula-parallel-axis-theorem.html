Parallel axis theorem — combining a compound weapon's parts into one total MOI

The general rule for adding up the MOI of a multi-part weapon (a disc plus a bolted-on tooth, a hub plus inserts, etc.): each part's own MOI about its own center of mass, plus that part's mass times the square of how far its center of mass sits from the actual rotation axis. Summing this over every part gives the true total MOI used in every KE/tip-speed/spin-up calculation.

Expression

I_axis = I_cm + m * d^2 ; I_total = sum over all parts of (I_cm_part + m_part * d_part^2)

Variables

I_axismoment of inertia of one part about the actual rotation axis, kg*m^2
I_cmthat part's own moment of inertia about a parallel axis through its own center of mass, kg*m^2
mmass of that part, kg
dperpendicular distance from the part's center of mass to the actual rotation axis, m

Worked example

A compound vertical-spinner weapon = a solid disc (from formula-moi-solid-disc: I_disc = 2.35e-5 kgm^2, already about the rotation axis since the disc is centered on it) plus one bolt-on steel tooth treated as a point mass m_tooth = 0.010 kg (10 g) whose own I_cm is negligible, bolted at d = 0.030 m from center. Tooth contribution = I_cm + md^2 = 0 + 0.010 0.030^2 = 0.010 0.0009 = 0.000009 kgm^2 (9.0e-6 kgm^2). I_total = 2.35e-5 + 9.0e-6 = 3.25e-5 kgm^2 -- this compound-weapon total (3.25e-5 kgm^2) is the number used in every rotational-KE, tip-speed and spin-up example elsewhere on this page.
