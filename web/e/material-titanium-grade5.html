Titanium Grade 5 (Ti-6Al-4V)

Aerospace-grade alpha-beta titanium alloy (6% aluminum, 4% vanadium) and the single most popular armor material in antweight and beetleweight combat robotics, prized for its extreme strength-to-weight ratio.

Materialantweightplastic-antweightarmortitaniumweapon-kingchassis

Strengths

  • Best strength-to-weight of any practical antweight armor metal
  • Roughly half the density of steel yet comparable or better strength
  • Excellent corrosion resistance, never rusts
  • Thin sheet (0.5-0.8mm) can be dimpled/formed to add rigidity without much weight

Weaknesses

  • Expensive — hobby-quantity sheet often $80-150/kg equivalent, far above bulk mill pricing
  • More brittle than mild/spring steel: tends to crack or shear cleanly under a hard hit rather than dent
  • Chews up cheap tooling; genuinely hard to cut and drill without carbide
  • Titanium dust is a fire hazard when sanding/grinding — do it wet or with dust extraction

Notes

For 1 lb antweights the standard playbook is: 0.5mm titanium wrapped/formed as a springy shock-absorbing skin around wheels or as a spring-steel-like flexible skirt; 0.8-1.0mm for rigid wedge plates and armor panels bolted to a plastic or aluminum frame; 1.2-1.5mm reserved for weight-tolerant beetleweight-adjacent 1lb builds or small armor patches over a weapon motor. Titanium's weakness is concentrated, repeated hits in the same spot — vertical spinners and hard hitting hammers can eventually crack a Ti plate that would survive a single hit fine. Cost per kg varies wildly by vendor; small hobby-cut sheets from suppliers like FingerTech, Combat Robotics NZ, and PalmBeachBots run well above raw mill-plate pricing because of cutting/shipping small orders.
