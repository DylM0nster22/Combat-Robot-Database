Dead Shaft

A weapon-mounting scheme where the shaft itself is rigidly bolted to the robot's frame and does not rotate; the weapon (a disc, drum, or 'bell') spins around the stationary shaft on its own bearings, usually driven by a pulley or sprocket bonded/bolted to the weapon body. Common on antweight belt-driven spinners because it lets the bearings sit directly inside the weapon, close to the mass, and removes the need for a rotating shaft to be supported by frame-mounted bearings on both sides.

Notes

Advantages: no need to machine a keyed rotating shaft, easier to swap a weapon belt in the pits since the hub simply lifts off the fixed shaft, and impact loads are reacted directly into the frame rather than through motor bearings. Disadvantage: the dead shaft's mounting screws must be re-torqued carefully every time it's removed, since a loose dead shaft binds and generates heat in the hub bearings.
