Why compliance (a longer stopping time) reduces peak impact force

For the same momentum change, spreading the impact over a longer contact time (via a compliant mount, standoff, or slightly flexible armor) directly and proportionally lowers the peak force -- the physical reason 'a little give' in a mount often survives hits that a rigid one cracks under.

Expression

F_peak_compliant = delta_p / delta_t_compliant , where delta_t_compliant > delta_t_rigid

Variables

F_peak_compliantpeak force with the more compliant mounting, N
delta_pmomentum change (same regardless of mounting stiffness), kg*m/s
delta_t_compliantlonger contact/deceleration time provided by the compliant mount, s

Worked example

Same delta_p = 0.3174 kg*m/s as formula-impact-force-momentum-time. Rigid mount: delta_t = 0.0015 s gives F_peak = 211.6 N. A compliant standoff (rubber bushing, slight frame flex) doubles the stopping time to delta_t = 0.0030 s: F_peak = 0.3174 / 0.0030 = 105.8 N -- exactly half the peak force, for the same impact.
