Mass-toward-the-rim effect on MOI

For a fixed total mass and fixed outer radius, moving material from the center toward the rim increases moment of inertia and stored energy without adding weight -- the single biggest lever in weapon geometry.

Formulaantweightplastic-antweightmoienergyrim-loadingdiscshell-spinner

Expression

I_disc = 0.5*m*R^2 (uniform disc);  I_annulus = 0.5*m*(R_out^2 + R_in^2) (rim-loaded ring of same mass & R_out);  I_thin_ring = m*R^2 (all mass at the rim)

Variables

mtotal weapon mass, kg
Router radius, m
R_ininner radius of the annulus (where the rim material starts), m
R_outouter radius, m

Worked example

80 g (0.080 kg) weapon, outer radius 32 mm. Uniform solid disc: I = 0.50.0800.032^2 = 4.10e-5 kgm^2. Same mass reshaped into an 8 mm-thick rim (R_in=24mm, R_out=32mm): I = 0.50.080(0.032^2+0.024^2) = 0.50.0800.0016 = 6.40e-5 kgm^2 -- 56% more MOI (and therefore 56% more stored energy at the same rpm) for the identical mass and outer diameter. Taken to the limit of an infinitely thin rim (a shell/ring spinner), I = mR^2 = 0.0800.032^2 = 8.19e-5 kg*m^2 -- exactly double the uniform disc.
