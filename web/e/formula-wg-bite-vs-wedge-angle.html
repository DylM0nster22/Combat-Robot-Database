Effective bite against an angled wedge surface

Striking a sloped wedge face instead of a flat vertical face reduces the normal (penetrating) component of a spinner's bite, which is why steep, aggressive wedges are an effective spinner counter and low, shallow wedges preserve more of the attacker's bite.

Expression

effective_bite ~= bite_depth * (1 - sin(wedge_angle))  (approximation; wedge_angle measured from horizontal, 0 = flat ground-level wedge, 90 = vertical flat face)

Variables

bite_depthcomputed bite depth against a flat vertical face, mm
wedge_angleangle of the opponent's wedge face from horizontal, degrees
effective_biteapproximate normal penetration achieved against the sloped face, mm

Worked example

A weapon with 4.5 mm of computed bite hitting a shallow 10 degree wedge: effective_bite ~= 4.5(1-sin(10)) = 4.5(1-0.174) = 3.72 mm -- most of the bite survives. The same weapon hitting a steep 40 degree wedge lip: effective_bite ~= 4.5(1-sin(40)) = 4.5(1-0.643) = 1.61 mm -- roughly a third of the flat-face bite, and in practice the tooth is also likely to deflect and ride up the face rather than dig in, which is the mechanism behind wedges surviving or even flipping spinners. This is a rough heuristic, not a rigorous contact-mechanics result, and real outcomes depend heavily on closing speed, ground clearance, and whether the wedge lip itself gets caught.
