N-Series (N10/N20/N30) Gearbox: Metal vs Plastic Gear Stages

A comparison entity describing the inconsistent internal construction of budget N-series gearmotors: many listings advertise "metal gear" but only some internal stages are actually metal, with the rest plastic, and quality varies not just by listing but by individual unit within the same order — the core of the antweight community's "quality lottery" problem with these motors.

Componentantweightplastic-antweightgearboxn-seriesquality-lotterybudget

Strengths

  • When a unit is good, it is very cheap for the torque delivered
  • All-metal-gear versions do exist and can be nearly as durable as name-brand options at a fraction of the cost

Weaknesses

  • No reliable way to verify actual gear material from a listing photo or description alone
  • Reported failure modes include stripped final-stage gears, inconsistent KV/no-load speed between "identical" units, and out-of-box dead units
  • No standardized manufacturer datasheet across the many resellers of what is often the same underlying OEM part

Notes

Community-standard mitigation: buy several spares from the start (treat them as a consumable), bench-test every motor before installing it (measure free-run current and no-load RPM, compare across the batch, and reject outliers), and avoid using an untested cheap N-series motor as the sole drive on a bot going into an important match. Name-brand options (FingerTech Silver/Gold Spark, Repeat Robotics, Pololu) cost more specifically because they remove this variance.
