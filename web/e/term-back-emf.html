Back-EMF (counter-electromotive force)

The voltage a spinning motor generates that opposes the applied drive voltage, proportional to RPM and inversely proportional to KV (Back-EMF volts = RPM / KV). As a weapon motor spins up, rising back-EMF is what makes available current -- and therefore torque -- fall off as speed increases, producing the classic linear torque-speed line.

Termglossaryback-emfmotor-electrical

Notes

At stall (RPM=0) back-EMF is zero, so the only thing limiting current is winding resistance -- this is why stall current is so high and why stalls generate the most heat. At no-load top speed, back-EMF has risen until it nearly equals the applied voltage, current drops toward zero, and torque drops toward zero too.
