UHMW-PE (Ultra-High Molecular Weight Polyethylene)

Extremely tough, low-friction thermoplastic that absorbs spinner impacts by deforming and stretching rather than cracking — a favorite ablative/energy-absorbing armor skin, especially over more brittle backing plates.

Materialantweightplastic-antweightarmorenergy-absorptionablativespinner-defense

Strengths

  • Exceptional impact toughness — extremely long molecular chains let it absorb huge amounts of energy by plastic deformation rather than fracturing
  • Very low coefficient of friction — spinner/blade hits tend to glance off rather than bite
  • Self-lubricating and abrasion resistant
  • Cheap and easy to cut/machine

Weaknesses

  • Almost impossible to glue reliably — its slick, low-surface-energy surface rejects most adhesives, so mounting means screws/rivets or specially surface-treated bonding
  • Low stiffness (low modulus) — deflects a lot under load, not good as a stand-alone rigid structural member
  • Will not stop a solid direct hit from a heavyweight-class spinner; primarily suited to lighter classes and side/rear armor rather than a full frontal spinner faceplate
  • Creeps (slowly deforms) under sustained load

Notes

UHMW's defining property is its molecular weight — chains so long they entangle and resist being pulled apart or fractured, so an impact's energy gets spent stretching and yielding polymer rather than propagating a crack. This makes it fundamentally different from a brittle plastic like acrylic or a stiff one like polycarbonate: instead of cracking or shattering, a UHMW panel dents, and often springs back. Common antweight thickness is 1/16" to 1/8" (1.6-3.2mm) as a side-armor skin or wheel-guard sleeve. The bonding problem is real enough that most builders mechanically fasten UHMW panels (screws through oversized/countersunk holes, or bolted-through-frame designs) rather than trying to glue them.
