Polycarbonate (Lexan)

Amorphous engineering thermoplastic with very high impact strength in sheet form — strong and clear, but sharply notch-sensitive, meaning small scratches, drilled holes, or sharp inside corners can trigger sudden cracking that undoes its toughness.

Materialantweightplastic-antweightarmornotch-sensitiveclear-plastic

Strengths

  • Very high unnotched impact strength — one of the toughest common clear/stock plastics
  • Good stiffness for a plastic
  • Reasonably priced and widely available in sheet form

Weaknesses

  • Severely notch-sensitive: a small nick, scratch, sharp corner, or an improperly drilled/tapped hole becomes a crack-initiation site that can propagate across the whole sheet under shock loading
  • As-extruded sheet carries internal stress that makes it more crack-prone until annealed
  • UV-sensitive over long term (yellows/embrittles) unless UV-stabilized grade
  • Scratches easily, and each scratch is a potential crack start

Notes

Two practical rules dominate using PC/Lexan well in combat robots: (1) anneal cut/machined parts (slow oven bake, e.g. ~125-130°C for an hour then slow cool) to relieve internal machining/forming stresses before competition — this significantly reduces spontaneous stress-cracking; (2) drill holes with a step drill or by first center-drilling and using a sharp bit at low speed/high feed with the sheet well-supported, never punch or force a hole, and always chamfer/de-burr edges — every sharp inside corner (including a hole) is a stress concentrator and Lexan will crack starting exactly there under a hard hit even though the flat, unnotched material is extremely tough.
