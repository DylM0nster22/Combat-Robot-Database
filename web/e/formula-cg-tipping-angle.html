Tipping angle from CG height and track/wheelbase

The lateral (or fore-aft) tilt angle at which a robot's center of gravity passes directly over its wheel contact line and it starts to roll over -- a low, wide robot with a low CG resists tipping far better than a tall, narrow one, which matters both for wedge rammer defense and for the gyro-dance check above.

Formulaantweightplastic-antweightweight-and-cgtippingstability

Expression

theta_tip = atan( (track_or_wheelbase / 2) / h_cg )

Variables

theta_tiptipping angle, rad (convert to degrees for intuition)
track_or_wheelbasedistance between the two wheel contact lines being tipped over (track width for side tipping, wheelbase for fore-aft tipping), m
h_cgheight of the center of gravity above the ground, m

Worked example

Low, wide build: track_width = 0.090 m, h_cg = 0.025 m (25mm, low battery/electronics stack). theta_tip = atan((0.090/2)/0.025) = atan(0.045/0.025) = atan(1.8) = 60.9 deg -- very stable, hard to tip. Tall, narrow build for contrast: track_width = 0.060 m, h_cg = 0.040 m (40mm, stacked components). theta_tip = atan((0.060/2)/0.040) = atan(0.03/0.04) = atan(0.75) = 36.9 deg -- noticeably easier to tip over from a side hit or an aggressive turn.
