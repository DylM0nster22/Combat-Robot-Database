Acceleration from motor torque (torque-limited case)

Converts drive-motor output torque, through the gearbox and wheel radius, into a forward driving force, and from there into acceleration -- BEFORE checking whether the wheels can actually put that much force onto the arena floor (see formula-traction-limited-force, which usually governs instead for lightweight antweights).

Formulaantweightplastic-antweightdrivetrainaccelerationtorque

Expression

F_drive = (T_motor * gear_ratio * eta) / r_wheel ; a = (F_drive * n_wheels) / m_robot

Variables

F_drivedriving force delivered at one wheel's contact patch, N
T_motormotor output (shaft) torque before the gearbox, N*m
gear_ratiogearbox reduction, dimensionless
etadrivetrain mechanical efficiency, dimensionless (0-1; typical small spur gearbox ~0.75-0.85)
r_wheelwheel radius, m
n_wheelsnumber of independently driven wheels contributing force
m_robottotal robot mass, kg
aresulting acceleration, m/s^2 (torque-limited, ignoring traction)

Worked example

T_motor = 0.010 Nm (10 mNm), gear_ratio = 20, eta = 0.80, r_wheel = 0.016 m (32mm dia wheel), n_wheels = 2, m_robot = 0.454 kg. F_drive (per wheel) = (0.010 20 0.80) / 0.016 = 0.16 / 0.016 = 10.0 N. Total force = 10.0 * 2 = 20.0 N. a = 20.0 / 0.454 = 44.1 m/s^2 IF the wheels could grip that hard -- see formula-traction-limited-force for why they usually can't, and formula-wheel-slip-threshold for the real limit.
