Plastic Ant Vertical Spinner

The single most common competitive plastic-ant archetype: a spinning disc or drum on a vertical (side-to-side relative to the arena floor, striking-face-forward) axis, printed from PETG or ABS, driven by a small brushless outrunner. Dominant because a thick printed disc keeps its weapon mass close to the axis and hits with blunt, ring-shaped force rather than requiring a hardened cutting edge.

Strengths

  • Printed disc/hub is simple to design and cheap to replace after a broken tooth or crack
  • Blunt ring impact matches what plastic can survive better than a thin blade
  • Good self-righting behavior if the disc doubles as a bumper

Weaknesses

  • Printed weapon can crack or delaminate at the bore/keyway under repeated hits
  • Needs a well-designed containment/guard since a plastic disc can shatter more unpredictably than metal
  • Kinetic energy per gram is inherently lower than a metal disc of the same size

Notes

Because printed plastic is roughly 3-6x less dense and far weaker in tension than hardened steel, plastic-ant vertical spinners spend a disproportionate share of the 454g budget on the disc/hub and its surrounding shroud rather than on drive power.
