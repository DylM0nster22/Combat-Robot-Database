Hub fastener shear capacity vs weapon torque

The torque a bolted or set-screw hub can transmit is set by the shear strength of its fasteners times their number and bolt-circle radius; comparing this to the weapon motor's stall torque shows whether the hub -- or something else -- is the weak link.

Expression

F_per_fastener = shear_stress_allow * area_shear;  T_capacity = N_fasteners * F_per_fastener * r_bolt_circle

Variables

shear_stress_allowallowable shear stress of the fastener material, Pa
area_shearshear cross-section area of one fastener, m^2
N_fastenersnumber of fasteners on the bolt circle
r_bolt_circleradius of the bolt circle, m
T_capacitytorque the hub joint can transmit before fastener shear failure, N*m

Worked example

A clamped shell-spinner ring held by 4x M2.5 steel screws (minor diameter ~2.0 mm, area = pi/40.002^2 = 3.14e-6 m^2) on a 15 mm bolt circle, allowable shear stress ~200 MPa: F_per_fastener = 3.14e-6200e6 = 628 N; T_capacity = 46280.015 = 37.7 Nm. A typical antweight weapon motor's stall torque is only about 0.05-0.3 Nm, so the bolted joint itself has a huge margin -- in practice, hub failures on ants are almost always set-screw slip on a smooth round shaft (a friction joint, not a shear joint) rather than fastener shear, which is why keyways and hex broaches are preferred once torque or shock loads get serious.
