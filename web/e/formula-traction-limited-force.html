Traction-limited pushing/driving force

The maximum forward (or pushing) force a robot's drive wheels can transmit to the floor before they simply spin in place, set by the robot's weight on the wheels and the wheel-to-floor friction coefficient -- NOT by how much torque the motors can produce. For a 454 g robot this is almost always the true limiting factor, well below what the drivetrain can output.

Formulaantweightplastic-antweightdrivetraintractionfriction

Expression

F_max = mu * N

Variables

F_maxmaximum available traction force, N
mucoefficient of friction between wheel and arena floor, dimensionless (foam/rubber on polycarbonate/Lexan: roughly 0.6-1.0)
Nnormal force pressing the drive wheels onto the floor, N (approximately the full robot weight if all wheels are driven)

Worked example

A 0.454 kg antweight, all weight on driven wheels: N = 0.454 9.81 = 4.453 N. Using mu = 0.8 (grippy foam wheel on a typical arena floor): F_max = 0.8 4.453 = 3.56 N. Compare to the 20.0 N the drivetrain could produce in formula-acceleration-from-torque -- the wheels will spin/slip long before the motors run out of torque.
