Rotational kinetic energy stored in a spinning weapon

Computes the joules of kinetic energy stored in a weapon flywheel from its moment of inertia and angular velocity -- the number that actually predicts damage potential, not RPM alone.

Formulaformulakinetic-energymoi

Expression

KE_J = 0.5 * I_kgm2 * omega_rads^2   where omega_rads = RPM * pi / 30

Variables

KE_Jstored rotational kinetic energy, joules
I_kgm2weapon moment of inertia about its spin axis, kg*m^2
omega_radsangular velocity, radians/second
RPMweapon speed, revolutions per minute

Worked example

A 150 g (0.15 kg) solid steel disc, 4 in (0.0508 m radius), approximated as a uniform disc: I = 0.5 m r^2 = 0.5 0.15 0.0508^2 = 0.0001935 kgm^2. At 21,000 RPM: omega = 21000 pi / 30 = 2199 rad/s. KE = 0.5 0.0001935 2199^2 = 0.5 0.0001935 4,835,600 approx 468 J. Typical antweight vertical-disc spinners store roughly 100-500 J at full speed, so this is a fairly strong hit for the class.
