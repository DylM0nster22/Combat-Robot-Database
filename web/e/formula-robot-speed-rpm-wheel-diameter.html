Robot Speed from Motor RPM and Wheel Diameter

Converts a drivetrain's output (wheel) RPM and wheel diameter into a theoretical no-load top speed, the first step in sizing any antweight drivetrain.

Formulaantweightplastic-antweightformulaspeedgear-ratiowheel

Expression

v_mph = (motor_rpm_no_load / gear_ratio) * pi * wheel_dia_in / 1056

Variables

motor_rpm_no_loadno-load RPM of the bare motor at the chosen nominal voltage
gear_ratiototal gearbox reduction, expressed as N:1 (use N)
wheel_dia_inwheel outer diameter in inches
v_mphtheoretical no-load top speed in miles per hour (real-world loaded speed is lower)

Worked example

FingerTech Silver Spark at 11.1:1 (motor_rpm_no_load = 11,530 at 7.4V) with a 2.0in wheel: wheel RPM = 11,530 / 11.1 = 1,038.7 RPM. v_mph = 1,038.7 x pi x 2.0 / 1056 = 1,038.7 x 6.283 / 1056 ~= 6.18 mph. This matches commonly reported antweight sprint speeds of roughly 6 mph. Real-world speed under load (current draw sagging the battery voltage, rolling resistance, and wheel slip) typically comes in at ~70-90% of this theoretical number.
