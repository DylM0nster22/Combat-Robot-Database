Carbon Fiber Plate (CF/Epoxy Laminate)

Woven or unidirectional carbon fiber cloth in an epoxy matrix, pressed into rigid sheet — extremely stiff and light, but catastrophically brittle: once its resin matrix cracks or fibers fail, it shatters/delaminates rather than deforming.

Materialantweightplastic-antweightcompositecarbon-fiberstiffbrittle

Strengths

  • Outstanding stiffness-to-weight ratio — among the lightest rigid plate options
  • Very light for its rigidity
  • Looks great and machines to tight tolerances with the right tooling

Weaknesses

  • Brittle: absorbs almost no energy plastically — a hard direct hit tends to shatter/delaminate the laminate rather than dent it
  • Conductive carbon dust/debris from a shattered plate can short out electronics nearby — a real secondary hazard in an arena hit
  • Expensive per kg
  • Poor choice as primary spinner-facing armor despite its popularity in hobbyist marketing

Notes

Carbon fiber plate's stiffness comes from very strong, very stiff fibers bonded in a relatively brittle epoxy matrix; under a hard impact the resin cracks first, and once cracked the fibers delaminate from each other and lose their combined strength almost instantly, so the panel goes from "very strong" to "structurally gone" in a single hit rather than gradually denting. This is why experienced builders reserve CF plate for stiffening/structural roles that are not the primary impact face (e.g., a top deck or an internal support rib), rather than for a front bumper or spinner-facing armor panel where UHMW, titanium, or TPU handle impact far better.
