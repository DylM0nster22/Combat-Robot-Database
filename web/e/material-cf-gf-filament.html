Carbon/Glass-Fiber-Filled Filament (PLA-CF, PETG-CF, generic)

Chopped carbon or glass fiber blended into a base filament (PLA, PETG, nylon) to dramatically increase stiffness — parts print much more rigid and dimensionally stable, but the fibers make the material more brittle in impact and are highly abrasive to standard brass nozzles.

Strengths

  • Stiffness increase of roughly 200-500% over the base unfilled polymer, per print-orientation-dependent testing — dramatically reduces flex/sag in long unsupported spans
  • Better dimensional stability/less warping than some unfilled bases
  • Looks and machines (post-print, lightly) nicely

Weaknesses

  • Fibers act as crack-initiation sites under impact — CF-filled parts are noticeably more brittle than their unfilled base polymer despite being stiffer; a hard direct hit is more likely to shatter/crack a CF part than deform it
  • Very abrasive to nozzles — ruins a standard brass nozzle quickly, mandatory hardened/ruby nozzle adds cost and complexity
  • Tensile strength gains are modest and orientation-dependent (often only 20-100% over base polymer in-plane) even though stiffness gains are large — builders sometimes overestimate real-world strength gain from the stiffness numbers alone
  • More expensive than unfilled base filament

Notes

The core engineering tradeoff with any short-fiber-filled filament is stiffness vs. toughness: the chopped fibers carry load very efficiently along their length (hence the big jump in elastic modulus), but each fiber end embedded in the matrix is a stress concentrator, and the fiber-matrix interface is a weak plane for crack propagation, so the composite as a whole loses ductility relative to the pure polymer. In a combat robot this means CF/GF-filled filament is excellent for parts that need to resist bending or sagging under steady load (long weapon-motor mount arms, frame spines) but a poor choice for a direct impact face, where the base unfilled polymer (or a compliant TPU skin) will usually survive a hit better than the "stronger-sounding" fiber-filled version.
