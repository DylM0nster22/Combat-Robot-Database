Nylon Filament (PA6 / PA12 / PA-CF)

3D-printable nylon in unfilled (PA6/PA12) and carbon-fiber-reinforced (PA-CF) forms — outstanding toughness, abrasion resistance, and fatigue life, but hygroscopic and finicky to print, so less common as full armor shells than as gears, hinges, and reinforced structural parts.

Materialantweightplastic-antweight3d-printedtoughhygroscopiccarbon-fiber-reinforced

Strengths

  • Best all-round toughness and abrasion resistance of common printables — high elongation-to-break (unfilled) means it flexes and absorbs energy rather than cracking
  • Excellent fatigue resistance — good for living hinges/latches that flex repeatedly
  • PA-CF variant adds major stiffness (tensile strength up to ~90-150 MPa depending on brand/fiber content) for structural parts that shouldn't flex

Weaknesses

  • Strongly hygroscopic — absorbs moisture from air within hours, which causes poor layer adhesion, stringing, and weaker parts unless printed from a dry-box/dryer
  • Warps significantly without a heated enclosure
  • PA-CF is markedly more brittle than unfilled nylon and very abrasive to nozzles — requires a hardened steel or ruby-tipped nozzle or the brass wears out fast
  • More expensive than PLA/PETG/ABS

Notes

Unfilled nylon is arguably the toughest common filament for a part that needs to survive repeated flexing or shock without cracking (hinges, drivetrain mounts, weapon retention clips), but its moisture sensitivity and warping make it less popular than PLA for full armor shells in casual antweight builds — it shows up more in mechanisms than outer armor. PA-CF trades away some of that ductility for stiffness and is used more like a lightweight aluminum substitute for frame rails than as an impact-absorbing armor skin.
