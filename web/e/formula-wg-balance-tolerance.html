Residual unbalance and shaking force vs RPM

A small leftover unbalance (mass times its offset from the rotation axis) produces a centrifugal shaking force that grows with the square of RPM, which is why fast antweight spinners need much tighter balance tolerances than slow ones.

Formulaantweightplastic-antweightbalancevibrationbearings

Expression

U = m_unbalance * r_offset (residual unbalance, g*mm);  F_shake = U(in kg*m) * omega^2 (centrifugal shaking force, N)

Variables

m_unbalanceleftover unbalanced mass, kg
r_offsetdistance of that mass from the rotation axis, m
omegaangular velocity, rad/s
Uresidual unbalance, conventionally expressed in gram-millimeters (g*mm)
F_shakerotating shaking force transmitted into the shaft and bearings, N

Worked example

A 150 g disc balanced to within 0.1 g at a 30 mm offset has U = 0.1 g 30 mm = 3 gmm = 3e-6 kgm. At 20,000 rpm (omega=2094.4 rad/s): F_shake = 3e-6 2094.4^2 = 3e-6 * 4.386e6 = 13.2 N (about 1.3 kgf) of rotating force reversing direction on every revolution. Doubling the RPM to 40,000 quadruples that to about 52.7 N for the identical residual unbalance -- the reason higher-speed ant spinners need noticeably tighter balance than slower ones, and why a weapon that felt fine at half throttle can shake a bot apart at full speed.
