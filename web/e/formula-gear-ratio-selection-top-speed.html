Selecting a Gear Ratio from a Desired Top Speed

Rearranges the speed formula to solve for the gear ratio needed to hit a target top speed with a given motor and wheel diameter, then checks the result against the ratios actually available off the shelf.

Formulaantweightplastic-antweightformulagear-ratiospeeddrivetrain-sizing

Expression

gear_ratio = motor_rpm_no_load * pi * wheel_dia_in / (target_mph * 1056)

Variables

motor_rpm_no_loadno-load RPM of the bare motor at the chosen nominal voltage
wheel_dia_inwheel outer diameter in inches
target_mphdesired no-load top speed, mph
gear_ratiogearbox reduction ratio needed, N:1 (solve for N)

Worked example

Target: 8 mph top speed using a Silver Spark motor (motor_rpm_no_load = 11,530 at 7.4V) and a 2.0in wheel. gear_ratio = 11,530 x pi x 2.0 / (8 x 1056) = 72,444 / 8,448 ~= 8.6:1. Silver Spark's fastest stock ratio is 11.1:1, not 8.6:1, so an exact 8 mph target isn't reachable with this motor/wheel pairing off the shelf. Two fixes: (1) increase wheel diameter to 2.5in and re-check the stock 11.1:1 ratio: wheel RPM = 11,530/11.1 = 1,038.7, v_mph = 1,038.7 x pi x 2.5 / 1056 ~= 7.7 mph, very close to the 8 mph target; or (2) switch to a higher-RPM brushless drive pod (e.g. Repeat Robotics' 1106 gearmotor), which can exceed this speed range easily by running a higher supply voltage (3S/4S) instead of changing wheel size.
