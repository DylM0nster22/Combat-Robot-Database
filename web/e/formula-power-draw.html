Weapon motor peak and steady electrical power draw

Estimates electrical power draw during weapon spin-up (near stall) versus steady-state windmilling, used to size battery C-rating and ESC current headroom.

Formulaformulapowerthermalesc-sizing

Expression

P_elec_W = V_applied * I_draw   |   P_mech_W = tau_Nm * omega_rads   |   P_copper_loss_W = I_draw^2 * R_ohm

Variables

P_elec_Welectrical power drawn from the battery, watts
V_appliedvoltage applied to the motor (approx pack voltage minus ESC/wiring drop), volts
I_drawcurrent draw, amps
P_mech_Wmechanical power delivered to the weapon shaft, watts
tau_Nmtorque at that instant, Nm
omega_radsangular velocity at that instant, rad/s
P_copper_loss_Wpower lost to heat in the windings, watts
R_ohmmotor phase-to-phase winding resistance, ohms

Worked example

2207 weapon motor, 1950kv, R=0.06 ohm, near stall on a 4S pack (14.8V nominal) pulling 30A: P_elec = 14.8 30 = 444 W peak electrical draw. Copper loss at that same 30A: P_copper = 30^2 0.06 = 54 W dissipated as heat in the stator -- a meaningful fraction of the 444 W, which is why repeated hard stalls (weapon hitting the floor/wall/opponent and stopping) heat the stator far faster than free-spinning. Once at full RPM with only bearing/aero drag to overcome, steady electrical draw for the same motor is typically only 5-20 W, which is why spin-up, not cruising, is what drains an antweight pack fastest.
