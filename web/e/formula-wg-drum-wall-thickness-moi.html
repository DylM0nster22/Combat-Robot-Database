Drum wall thickness vs MOI (thin-wall vs thick-wall drum)

For a fixed drum outer diameter and total mass, a thinner wall (mass pushed toward the outside) gives meaningfully more MOI than a thick-walled drum of the same weight -- the drum-spinner version of the mass-toward-rim principle.

Formulaantweightplastic-antweightmoidrumwall-thickness

Expression

I = 0.5*m*(R_out^2 + R_in^2) for a thick-walled cylinder about its own axis; as R_in approaches R_out (thin wall) this approaches I = m*R_out^2

Variables

mdrum mass, kg
R_outouter radius, m
R_ininner (bore) radius, m

Worked example

100 g drum, 40 mm OD (R_out=0.020 m). Thin-wall version, 3 mm wall (R_in=0.017 m): I = 0.50.100(0.020^2+0.017^2) = 0.50.1000.000689 = 3.445e-5 kgm^2. Thick-wall version, 10 mm wall (R_in=0.010 m), same OD and same total mass: I = 0.50.100(0.0004+0.0001) = 2.50e-5 kgm^2. The thin-wall drum carries 38% more MOI for the identical mass and outer diameter, which is why competitive antweight drums use a thin, hardened outer shell (often with a lighter core/spine) rather than a solid steel cylinder.
