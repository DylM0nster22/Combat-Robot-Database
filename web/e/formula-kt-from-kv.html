Torque constant (Kt) from velocity constant (Kv)

For a sinusoidal-drive brushless motor, torque constant in Nm/A is derived directly from the velocity constant in RPM/V using the fixed 8.27 relationship.

Formulaformulamotor-constantstorque

Expression

Kt_Nm_per_A = 8.27 / Kv_rpm_per_v

Variables

Kt_Nm_per_Atorque constant, newton-meters per amp
Kv_rpm_per_vmotor velocity constant, RPM per volt

Worked example

A 2207 antweight weapon motor at 1950 kv: Kt = 8.27 / 1950 = 0.00424 Nm/A. At a hypothetical 30A near-stall current, instantaneous torque approx 0.00424 * 30 = 0.127 Nm (1.27 kgf-cm). Compare to a 2814 at 1850kv is similar Kt per amp, but its much higher max current (50A+) gives far higher absolute stall torque -- this is why stator volume, not KV alone, sets how much torque a motor can actually produce.
