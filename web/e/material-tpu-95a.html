TPU 95A (Rigid-Flex Thermoplastic Polyurethane)

The stiffest common Shore-A TPU grade, and arguably the single most important material development for plastic-antweight armor: a flexible, elastic skin that deforms under a spinner hit and springs back rather than transmitting or absorbing the blow as fracture damage.

Strengths

  • Absorbs and dissipates spinner/impact energy through large elastic deformation instead of transmitting shock into the rigid chassis or cracking
  • Extremely high elongation-to-break (400-500%+) — essentially un-crackable in normal combat impacts
  • Springs back to shape after a hit, so a single fight rarely destroys it the way a cracked rigid shell would
  • Stiffer/more print-friendly than softer 85A TPU while still very compliant compared to any rigid plastic

Weaknesses

  • Low absolute strength/stiffness — cannot be the sole structural member, needs a rigid frame underneath
  • Prints slower than rigid filaments and needs tuned retraction/speed settings
  • More expensive per kg than PLA/PETG

Notes

TPU95 armor works on a completely different principle than rigid armor: rather than resisting deformation (titanium, PLA) or absorbing energy through toughness before failing (UHMW, nylon), a TPU shell simply gets out of the way — a spinner tooth or hammer face compresses/stretches the rubber-like material, spreading the impact over a larger area and longer time, and the material relaxes back to shape afterward. This means a TPU-armored plastic antweight can often take many hits to the same panel without cumulative damage, unlike a rigid shell that accumulates cracks. The tradeoff is that TPU alone provides essentially no structural rigidity or drivetrain-mount strength, so practical builds use it as an outer skin (a "suit" wrapped around or over-molded/printed onto) a rigid PLA/PETG/nylon or metal frame that does the structural work while the TPU layer does the impact-absorbing work. This flexible-armor-over-rigid-frame approach is widely considered the state of the art for plastic-antweight armor as of the mid-2020s.
