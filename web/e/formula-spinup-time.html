Weapon spin-up time estimate from torque and MOI

Estimates the time to bring a weapon from rest to full speed given the weapon's moment of inertia and the motor's average available accelerating torque over that speed range -- the calculation that explains why an underpowered motor loses matches before the fight even starts.

Formulaformulaspin-up-timemoitorque

Expression

t_s (approx) = (I_kgm2 * omega_final_rads) / tau_avg_Nm   where tau_avg is roughly half of stall torque for a fixed-voltage DC/BLDC speed-torque line

Variables

t_sspin-up time, seconds
I_kgm2weapon-side moment of inertia (blade/disc + bell + hub, reflected through any gear ratio)
omega_final_radstarget angular velocity, rad/s
tau_avg_Nmaverage accelerating torque during spin-up, Nm (approx 0.5 x stall torque for a motor driven at constant voltage with a linear torque-speed curve)

Worked example

Same 0.0001935 kgm^2 disc targeting 2199 rad/s (21,000 RPM), driven direct-drive by a 2207 at 1950kv with an assumed 30A stall current: Kt = 8.27/1950 = 0.00424 Nm/A, so stall torque tau_stall = 0.0042430 = 0.127 Nm, and average torque during spin-up approx 0.50.127 = 0.0636 Nm. t = (0.0001935 2199) / 0.0636 = 0.4255 / 0.0636 approx 6.7 s. A 6-7 second spin-up is dangerously slow in a 3-minute antweight match -- this is exactly why builders either shrink the weapon MOI, raise motor torque (bigger stator / lower KV / more cells), or accept a slower-spinning but faster-spooling weapon.
