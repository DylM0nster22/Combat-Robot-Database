PLA / PLA+

Polylactic acid — the default, dominant 3D-print material in plastic antweights. Often dismissed as "brittle" because of its low elongation-to-break, but its high stiffness, ease of printing at high infill/wall counts, low cost, and excellent layer adhesion make it the single most-used structural and armor material in the class.

Strengths

  • Cheapest and easiest filament to print well — minimal warping, no enclosure needed, tight dimensional accuracy
  • High stiffness for a printed plastic — resists flexing/deformation under load, holds shape under motor/servo mounting stress
  • Excellent inter-layer adhesion when printed correctly, so printed shells are less prone to delaminating on impact than people assume
  • Cheap enough to be genuinely disposable — reprinting a cracked shell overnight is trivial and nearly free
  • PLA+ blends add modest toughness/impact-resistance over standard PLA at a small cost premium

Weaknesses

  • Genuinely low elongation-to-break and impact toughness compared to nylon or TPU — a sharp, hard hit can crack or shatter a thin PLA wall
  • Low heat deflection temperature (~55-60°C) — can soften/warp in a hot car or near a hot motor/ESC
  • More brittle in cold conditions

Notes

PLA dominates plastic-antweight construction for reasons beyond raw toughness numbers: it is the easiest material in existence to get a dimensionally accurate, well-adhered, high-infill part out of on a cheap printer with zero enclosure, it is inexpensive enough that builders print thick-walled, mostly-solid parts (60-100% infill) rather than thin shells, and a solid thick PLA part is often tougher in practice than a thin, high-elongation but weaker-walled alternative. Its low elongation is a real weakness against sustained flex, but a 1 lb spinner hit is usually a single, brief high-force event where PLA's stiffness (resisting deformation in the first place) matters more than its ductility. The community consensus is that PLA/PLA+ printed thick and reinforced with a rigid frame (or backed by a compliant material like TPU) beats most alternatives on a cost/performance/ease basis for plastic-antweight class, which is precisely why it remains the default choice even though on paper materials like nylon or ABS have better toughness metrics.
