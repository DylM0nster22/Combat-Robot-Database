Tungsten Carbide (WC-Co)

Cemented carbide (tungsten carbide grains in a cobalt binder), used as small hardened inserts/tips on weapon edges — extremely hard and wear-resistant but so brittle it chips or shatters under off-axis impact rather than deforming.

Materialantweightplastic-antweighthardened-insertweapon-tipextreme-hardness

Strengths

  • Hardest practical combat-robot material — extreme wear/abrasion resistance
  • Holds an edge/point far longer than any tool steel
  • Extremely stiff (high elastic modulus) so it resists deflection on contact

Weaknesses

  • Very low fracture toughness/tensile strength — chips or shatters under bending, shock, or off-center impact instead of yielding
  • Dense (nearly 15 g/cm3) — must be used in small inserts, not full plates, to stay in an antweight's budget
  • Expensive per kg and difficult to shape without specialized tooling
  • Impossible to repair in the pits; a chipped insert is simply gone

Notes

The core tradeoff with tungsten carbide is hardness vs toughness: hardness comes from a rigid crystal lattice that resists dislocation motion (the same mechanism that gives wear resistance), but that same rigidity means the material has almost no ability to absorb energy by plastic deformation. When a carbide tooth hits something slightly off-axis, the impact energy has nowhere to go except into crack propagation through the brittle carbide grains, so it chips or shears out in chunks rather than denting like a tougher steel would. This is why combat robot builders use carbide only as small brazed/bolted-on tips on a tough steel body (S7/4340), never as the entire weapon.
