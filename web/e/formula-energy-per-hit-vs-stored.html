Energy actually delivered in one hit, vs total energy stored

A weapon rarely dumps its entire stored KE into a single hit -- the disc only slows down by however much the impact actually resists it, and keeps most of its spin for the next hit. This computes the energy actually delivered from the RPM drop observed/expected during a strike.

Formulaantweightplastic-antweightrotationalkinetic-energyimpact

Expression

E_hit = 0.5 * I * (omega_before^2 - omega_after^2)

Variables

E_hitkinetic energy delivered to the target in one hit, J
Itotal weapon moment of inertia, kg*m^2
omega_beforeweapon angular velocity just before the hit, rad/s
omega_afterweapon angular velocity just after the hit, rad/s

Worked example

I = 3.25e-5 kgm^2, weapon at 20,000 rpm before the hit (omega_before = 2094.4 rad/s) drops to 17,000 rpm after (omega_after = 1780.2 rad/s). E_hit = 0.5 3.25e-5 (2094.4^2 - 1780.2^2) = 0.5 3.25e-5 (4,386,506 - 3,169,112) = 0.5 3.25e-5 1,217,394 = 0.5 39.57 = 19.8 J delivered -- only about 28% of the 71.3 J total stored KE from formula-rotational-ke. The rest stays in the still-spinning weapon for the next hit, and the ~1s spin-up time formula shows how quickly the lost 3,000 rpm can be recovered mid-fight.
