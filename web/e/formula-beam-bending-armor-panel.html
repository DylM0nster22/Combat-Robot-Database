Beam bending stress in a flat armor panel

Models an armor panel bolted at two edges as a simply-supported beam under a central point impact load, giving the peak bending stress at the panel's surface -- the standard first check for whether a given armor thickness/material will survive a hit without cracking or permanently bending.

Formulaantweightplastic-antweightstructurebeam-bendingarmor

Expression

M = F * L / 4 ; I_beam = b * t^3 / 12 ; sigma_max = M * c / I_beam , with c = t/2

Variables

Mbending moment at the panel's midspan, N*m
Fimpact force applied at midspan, N
Lspan between the panel's two support/mount points, m
I_beamsecond moment of area of the panel's cross-section, m^4
bpanel width, m
tpanel thickness, m
cdistance from the neutral axis to the outer surface (= t/2 for a symmetric panel), m
sigma_maxmaximum bending stress at the panel surface, Pa

Worked example

Aluminum armor panel: b = 0.050 m (50mm wide), t = 0.003 m (3mm thick), mounted with L = 0.040 m (40mm) between bolts, hit at midspan with F = 211.6 N. M = 211.6 0.040 / 4 = 8.464/4 = 2.116 Nm. I_beam = 0.050 0.003^3 / 12 = 0.050 0.000000027 / 12 = 0.00000000135/12 = 1.125e-10 m^4. c = 0.0015 m. sigma_max = 2.116 * 0.0015 / 1.125e-10 = 0.003174 / 1.125e-10 = 2.82e7 Pa = 28.2 MPa -- well under 6061-T6 aluminum's ~276 MPa yield strength (safety factor ~9.8x), so this panel survives the hit with margin to spare.
