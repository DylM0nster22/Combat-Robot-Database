Voltage sag from internal resistance under load

The battery's terminal voltage drops below its nominal/open-circuit voltage in proportion to the current drawn and the pack's internal resistance. Underestimating this leads to overestimating available torque/power right when it's needed most (weapon stall, hard push).

Formulaantweightplastic-antweightelectricalbatteryvoltage-saginternal-resistance

Expression

V_loaded = V_nominal - I_draw * R_internal

Variables

V_loadedactual terminal voltage under load, V
V_nominalnominal (open-circuit / resting) battery voltage, V
I_drawcurrent being drawn, A
R_internalbattery pack internal resistance, ohm

Worked example

2S LiPo, V_nominal = 7.4 V, R_internal = 0.020 ohm (20 mOhm, typical small high-C pack). At a modest I_draw = 10 A: V_loaded = 7.4 - 100.020 = 7.4 - 0.20 = 7.20 V. At the pack's rated max continuous I_draw = 18 A (from formula-battery-c-rating-to-amps): V_loaded = 7.4 - 180.020 = 7.4 - 0.36 = 7.04 V -- a ~5% sag right when the weapon needs full torque.
