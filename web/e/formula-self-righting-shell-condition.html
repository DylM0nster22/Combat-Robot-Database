Approximate self-righting condition for a rounded-shell design

A simplified rule of thumb for shell/dome-shaped robots (common on shell spinners and some invertible bots): if the robot's center-of-gravity height stays below the radius of its rounded shell, gravity alone tends to roll it back onto its wheels when flipped, rather than leaving it balanced or stuck upside-down.

Expression

self_rights_readily if h_cg < R_shell

Variables

h_cgheight of the center of gravity above the ground when the robot rests normally, m
R_shellradius of the rounded outer shell profile, m

Worked example

A shell-spinner has a domed shell of radius R_shell = 0.045 m (45mm) and its CG sits at h_cg = 0.030 m (30mm, low battery/motor placement). Since 0.030 < 0.045, the design satisfies the rule of thumb and should tend to self-right if flipped. If h_cg were instead 0.050 m (a top-heavy build), it would fail the check and risk resting stably upside-down.

Notes

A rough qualitative rule of thumb, not a rigorous rigid-body stability derivation; real self-righting also depends on shell shape uniformity, weapon drag, and floor friction.
