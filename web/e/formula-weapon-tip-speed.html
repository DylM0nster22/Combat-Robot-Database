Spinning Weapon Tip Speed

The standard combat-robotics formula for converting a spinning weapon's no-load RPM and diameter into tip speed in feet per second, taken verbatim from SPARC's Robot Construction Specifications (used to set/verify Sportsman-class and open-air tip-speed limits) and used informally by builders in every weight class, plastic ant included, to sanity-check a weapon design before it's built.

Expression

tip_speed_ft_s = no_load_rpm * diameter_in * 0.00436

Variables

tip_speed_ft_sweapon tip/rim speed at the outer edge, in feet per second
no_load_rpmthe weapon's unloaded (free-spinning, no-load) RPM as measured by tachometer or estimated from motor kv * voltage / gear or pulley ratio
diameter_inthe weapon's outer diameter in inches (for a disc/drum, its full diameter; for a bar, the full swept diameter tip-to-tip)

Worked example

A plastic-ant vertical spinner disc with a 3.5in printed diameter, driven by a 2600kv brushless motor on 2S (7.4V) direct-drive (no reduction), has a theoretical no-load RPM of about 2600 7.4 = 19,240 RPM. Applying the formula: tip_speed = 19,240 3.5 * 0.00436 ≈ 293 ft/s (about 200 mph) at the theoretical no-load ceiling. Real-world loaded RPM is always lower than this no-load figure (ESC/motor sag under load, printed-disc drag), so builders typically see tip speeds in the 120-220 ft/s (~80-150mph) band once the disc is actually spinning under battery sag, which is still enough kinetic energy to crack a printed panel or dent a wedge on a solid hit. For reference, SPARC's own Sportsman-class rule caps NON-toothed slow spinners at 20 ft/s using this exact formula, which shows how enormous the gap is between a 'slow spinner' and a full-power plastic-ant spinner: roughly a 10-15x difference in tip speed.

Notes

Builders should treat the no-load RPM figure as an upper bound only; a printed weapon's SURVIVABLE rpm (the ceiling before the weapon itself fails from centrifugal stress) is usually the true limiting factor in plastic ant, not motor Kv or battery voltage — see the printed-weapon burst-strength discussion in the build-spec chunks.
