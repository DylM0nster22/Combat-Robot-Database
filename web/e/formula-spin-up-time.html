Spin-up time from motor torque and weapon MOI

Estimates how long a weapon takes to reach operating speed from a standstill, using the average available motor torque over the acceleration and the weapon's total MOI. Long spin-up times are a real tactical liability (weapon is weak/inert while re-spinning after a hit).

Formulaantweightplastic-antweightrotationalspin-upelectricalmotor-sizing

Expression

t_spinup = I * delta_omega / T_avg

Variables

t_spinuptime to spin up, s
Itotal weapon moment of inertia, kg*m^2
delta_omegachange in angular velocity, rad/s (target minus starting, usually target minus 0)
T_avgaverage torque delivered by the motor over the spin-up range, N*m

Worked example

I_total = 3.25e-5 kgm^2, target omega = 2094.4 rad/s (20,000 rpm) from rest. Motor: Kv = 2750 rpm/V, V = 11.1 V (3S), stall current I_stall = 30 A. Kt (exact) = 9.5493 / 2750 = 0.003472 Nm/A. T_stall = Kt I_stall = 0.003472 30 = 0.1042 Nm. No-load rpm = Kv V = 2750 11.1 = 30,525 rpm (omega_nl = 3196 rad/s). Torque at the 20,000 rpm operating point (linear torque-speed curve): T(omega) = T_stall (1 - omega/omega_nl) = 0.1042 (1 - 2094.4/3196) = 0.1042 0.345 = 0.03595 Nm. T_avg (approx, midpoint of a linear torque curve) = (0.1042 + 0.03595) / 2 = 0.0701 Nm. t_spinup = 3.25e-5 * 2094.4 / 0.0701 = 0.06807 / 0.0701 = 0.97 s -- under one second, a good, fight-safe spin-up time.
