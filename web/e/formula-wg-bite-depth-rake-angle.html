Effective bite path length vs tooth rake angle

A swept-back (raked) tooth follows a longer path through the opponent's material for the same straight-line bite depth, trading peak impact force for a longer, lower-force engagement -- a simplified geometric model for comparing tooth profiles.

Expression

path_length ~= bite_depth / cos(rake_angle)  (approximation for a tooth face swept back by rake_angle from purely radial)

Variables

bite_depthstraight-line penetration per hit from the base bite formula, mm
rake_angleangle of the tooth's leading face from a purely radial line, degrees
path_lengthapproximate length of material actually engaged along the raked face, mm

Worked example

A tooth with 4.5 mm of computed bite depth (from formula-bite-depth in the design-math topic) and a 0 degree radial rake engages the full 4.5 mm as a direct impact. The same 4.5 mm bite on a tooth raked back 25 degrees engages path_length = 4.5/cos(25) = 4.5/0.906 = 4.97 mm of material -- about 10% more contact length -- but because the raked face sheds the hit more tangentially, peak instantaneous force on the tooth and bearings is lower. This is a simplified geometric approximation, not a rigorous impact model, and real behavior also depends on the opponent's material and how it fractures or deforms.
